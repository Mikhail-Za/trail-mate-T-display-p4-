Yarrow (Achillea millefolium): minor bleeding and wounds

== WHAT IT DOES ==
- Yarrow is an old battlefield wound herb, known as soldier's woundwort and the nosebleed plant.
- Crushed fresh leaves, pressed on the skin, can help slow minor bleeding and soothe small wounds.
- Topical first aid only. Direct pressure is what stops bleeding; yarrow is a helper, not a replacement.

== DEADLY LOOKALIKE: READ THIS FIRST ==
- Yarrow's feathery leaves resemble the carrot and parsley family, which includes POISON HEMLOCK and WATER HEMLOCK, among the deadliest plants in North America. A mistake here can kill.
- Because of that risk: if you cannot separate yarrow from the hemlocks with total confidence, DO NOT pick it. Use direct pressure and a plantain poultice instead.

== HOW TO TELL YARROW APART ==
- Smell: crushed yarrow has a strong, pleasant, sweet and spicy herbal scent. Poison hemlock smells foul, musty, and mouse-like. Trust a bad smell and stop.
- Leaves: yarrow leaves are soft and very finely divided, feathery like a fern or a feather, set directly along the stem. The name millefolium means thousand-leaf.
- Flowers: yarrow bears FLAT-TOPPED clusters of many tiny white (sometimes pink) flowers, each a small daisy. The hemlocks carry lacy, dome or umbrella-shaped clusters (umbels).
- Stem: yarrow stems are tough, green, and often slightly hairy or woolly. Poison hemlock stems are smooth, hairless, and blotched with purple. Purple blotches mean walk away.
- Size: yarrow is usually 1 to 3 feet tall. Poison hemlock towers to 6 to 8 feet.

== IDENTIFY YARROW ==
- Feathery, fern-like leaves in a basal cluster and up the stem.
- A single, mostly unbranched stem topped by a flat cluster of tiny white daisies.
- A strong, clean herbal aroma when crushed.
- Dry fields, roadsides, meadows, and trail edges across nearly all of North America.

== HOW TO USE ==
- Clean the wound and control bleeding with direct pressure first.
- Crush clean fresh leaves into a pulp and press them onto the minor wound or the outside of a bleeding nostril. Hold.

== CAUTION ==
- Topical only. Do not take it internally from this guide.
- Yarrow is in the aster and daisy family. People allergic to ragweed, chamomile, or daisies may react. Patch-test first.
- Avoid in pregnancy.
- If there is ANY doubt about the identity, do not use it. No wound benefit is worth a hemlock mistake.

== SEASON ==
- Leaves are usable spring through fall; flowers come in summer. Use fresh leaves.
