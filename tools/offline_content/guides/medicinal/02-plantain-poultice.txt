Plantain Poultice (Plantago species): stings, bites, minor wounds

== WHAT IT DOES ==
- Crushed plantain leaf, laid on the skin, soothes bug
  stings and bites, nettle sting, minor cuts, scrapes,
  and itch. It cools the spot and can ease swelling.
- Lab and animal studies show it has mild germ-slowing
  and anti-inflammatory action, and it may help small
  wounds close. Human proof is thin.
- Honest limit: it is a soothing helper, NOT a cure and
  NOT a substitute for cleaning a wound. Do not count on
  it to stop an infection. Topical use only.
- Its real value in the field: it is safe, everywhere,
  and free, so use it while you do the steps below.

== DO THIS FIRST: STING OR BITE ==
- Get away from the hive, nest, or swarm before treating.
- Bee stinger left in skin: get it OUT fast. Scrape it
  with a fingernail, knife back, or card, or just pluck
  it. Speed matters more than method. Do not waste time.
- Wash the spot with clean or disinfected water. Soap
  if you have it.
- Cool it: cold water soak, a cloth wet in a cold
  stream, or snow/ice wrapped in fabric. About 10 min
  on, 10 min off. This is your strongest pain and swell
  reliever.
- Raise the limb if it is swelling.
- Now apply plantain (see MAKE AND USE below) for itch
  and soothing. Reapply as it dries out.
- Do not scratch. Broken skin from scratching is how a
  simple bite turns into an infection.

== DO THIS FIRST: CUT OR SCRAPE ==
- Stop bleeding: firm direct pressure with the cleanest
  cloth you have, held hard for several minutes. Raise
  the part above the heart if you can.
- Clean is everything. Irrigate: flush the wound with a
  lot of clean or disinfected water, pushed in hard.
  Squeeze it from a bottle or a bag with a pinhole to
  make a jet. Use a half liter or more. Getting the dirt
  and germs OUT beats any ointment or leaf.
- Pick out visible grit and dead tissue.
- Leave a dirty, deep, or animal-bite wound OPEN. Do not
  seal it tight. Cover with a clean dressing.
- Plantain here is an add-on only, over a small clean
  wound, under the dressing. It does not replace the
  flush.
- Keep it covered, and re-clean and re-dress daily or
  when dirty or wet.

== MAKE AND USE THE POULTICE ==
- Clean the skin first.
- Crush a clean leaf to a green pulp: mash it between two
  clean rocks, roll it hard, or chew it (the old "spit
  poultice").
- For an OPEN wound, prefer crushing over chewing, so you
  do not add mouth germs. Chewing is fine for stings and
  itch on unbroken skin.
- Lay the pulp on the spot, hold it there, and refresh it
  every few hours or when it dries.
- For quick itch or nettle sting, just rub a crushed leaf
  on the skin.

== IDENTIFY: SEE THE EDIBLE ENTRY ==
- Same plant as Broadleaf Plantain in the edible section.
  Use that entry for full identification.
- Quick check: a low, ground-hugging rosette in trampled
  ground; broad oval leaves (or narrow leaves, for
  ribwort) with strong parallel veins that pull out as
  tough strings; a leafless rat-tail seed spike.
- Both broadleaf (Plantago major) and narrowleaf ribwort
  (Plantago lanceolata) work.

== LOOKALIKES ==
- Confirm all three together: stringy parallel veins, the
  flat rosette, and the rat-tail seed spike.
- Near shade or gardens, do not confuse young rosettes
  with lily of the valley or false hellebore, both
  DEADLY if eaten. See the edible entry. When unsure, do
  not use.

== IF YOU CANNOT REACH HELP: WOUND GOING BAD ==
Watch for spreading redness, growing pain, heat, pus, a
bad smell, or fever. Mark the edge of the redness with
ink or a mud line and check if it is growing. If it is,
work the whole list, not one item:
- Re-open and re-flush the wound hard with clean water.
  Repeat this often. Irrigation is your main weapon.
- If pus is trapped under a tight, tender, swollen bump,
  open the wound edge and let it drain. Trapped pus must
  come out. Flush after draining.
- Warm, clean, moist compresses several times a day to
  pull the infection to the surface and help it drain.
- Rest the part, raise it, keep it clean and dry between
  care.
- Drink water and eat if you can. A hydrated, rested body
  fights infection better.
- If you carry antibiotics, this is when they earn their
  place. Take a full course, do not stop early. Do not
  fake this with random pills or plants.
- Evacuate if you can safely reach help; carry this on
  the wound care above, do not wait idle for rescue.
Hard truth: spreading infection that reaches the blood
(red streaks up the limb, high fever, shaking chills,
confusion) can kill, and no plant fixes that. The steps
above buy time and may be enough for a minor infection.
They are not a guarantee.

== IF YOU CANNOT REACH HELP: SEVERE ALLERGIC REACTION ==
A sting or bite can trigger a body-wide reaction. This is
the true emergency, far more than the bite itself.
Signs (away from the bite site): spreading hives,
swelling of lips, tongue, or throat, tight throat, wheeze
or trouble breathing, vomiting, dizziness, feeling of
doom, collapse. This can kill in minutes.
- Epinephrine is THE treatment. If an auto-injector or
  vial is in any pack, use it at the FIRST sign, into the
  outer thigh muscle. Adult dose about 0.3 mg. Repeat in
  5 to 15 minutes if no better. Do not wait to "see how
  bad it gets." Waiting kills.
- Lay the person flat and raise the legs. If they are
  struggling to breathe, let them sit up. If vomiting or
  sleepy, roll them on their side. Do NOT let them stand
  or walk suddenly, that can cause fatal collapse.
- Adjuncts only, they do NOT replace epinephrine: an oral
  antihistamine like diphenhydramine (25 to 50 mg adult)
  if awake and able to swallow; a rescue inhaler
  (albuterol) for wheeze if carried.
- If they stop breathing, start rescue breaths and CPR.
- Evacuate or signal for help the instant you can while
  giving this care.
Hard truth: without epinephrine there is NO reliable
field substitute. Antihistamines are too slow and do not
reverse throat swelling or shock. Plantain does nothing
for this. Positioning, an antihistamine, an inhaler, and
rescue breathing are all you have, and they may not be
enough. Anyone with a known sting allergy must carry an
epinephrine auto-injector into the field. Every time.

== CAUTION ==
- Topical first aid only, for minor injuries.
- Do not pack plantain into deep wounds, and do not let
  it replace flushing and cleaning.
- Allergy to plantain is rare but possible. Stop if the
  irritation gets worse.
- If chewing the leaf, use a clean mouth and clean water,
  and skip chewing if you have mouth sores.

== SEASON ==
- Leaves are available spring through fall. Use fresh.
