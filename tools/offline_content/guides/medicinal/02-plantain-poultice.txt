Plantain Poultice (Plantago species): stings, bites, minor wounds

== WHAT IT DOES ==
- Crushed plantain leaf, applied to the skin, soothes insect stings and bites, nettle sting, minor cuts and scrapes, and itch.
- It may help ease swelling and draw at splinters or stingers.
- It is one of the safest and most widespread field remedies. Topical use only.

== IDENTIFY: SEE THE EDIBLE ENTRY ==
- This is the same plant as Broadleaf Plantain in the edible section. Use that entry for full identification.
- Quick check: a low, ground-hugging rosette in trampled ground; broad oval leaves (or narrow leaves, for ribwort plantain) with strong parallel veins that pull out as tough strings; a leafless rat-tail seed spike.
- Both broadleaf plantain (Plantago major) and narrowleaf ribwort (Plantago lanceolata) work.

== LOOKALIKES ==
- Even for skin use, confirm the stringy parallel veins, the flat rosette, and the rat-tail seed spike together.
- Near shade or gardens, do not confuse young rosettes with lily of the valley or false hellebore, both DEADLY. See the edible entry. When unsure, do not use.

== HOW TO USE ==
- Clean the skin first.
- Crush or chew a clean leaf into a green pulp (the old "spit poultice") and lay it on the sting or scrape.
- Hold it in place and refresh it every few hours.
- For quick itch or sting relief, rub a crushed leaf directly on the spot.

== CAUTION ==
- Topical first aid only, for minor injuries.
- Do not pack it into deep wounds. Control bleeding and clean first (see the first-aid section).
- Allergy is rare but possible. Stop if it makes the irritation worse.
- If chewing the leaf, use a clean mouth and clean water, and skip chewing if you have mouth sores.

== SEASON ==
- Leaves are available spring through fall. Use them fresh.
