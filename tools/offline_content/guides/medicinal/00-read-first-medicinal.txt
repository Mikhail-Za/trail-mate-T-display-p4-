READ FIRST: Medicinal and First-Aid Plants

== WHAT THIS SECTION IS ==
- These are field first-aid folk remedies, not medicine. They are comfort and stopgap measures for minor problems when nothing better is at hand.
- Like the rest of this guide, it is a memory aid for things you already know, not a substitute for training.
- Everything here leans topical (on the skin) on purpose. Only two entries are taken internally, willow bark and pine needle tea, and both carry heavy warnings.

== NOT A SUBSTITUTE FOR REAL MEDICAL CARE ==
- A plant remedy does not fix a serious injury or illness. It buys a little comfort, not a cure.
- For deep wounds, heavy bleeding, bad burns, infection, broken bones, chest or belly pain, trouble breathing, or anything worsening: treat the basics and evacuate to real care.
- Control bleeding and clean the wound FIRST (see the first-aid section). A remedy comes second, if at all.

== THE ID RULE STILL APPLIES ==
- Never take any plant internally unless you are 100 percent certain of its identity.
- The foraging rules still hold here: no wild mushrooms, avoid the carrot and parsley family, and when unsure, do nothing.
- Wrong plant on the skin can still harm you. Poison hemlock and others are dangerous even to handle.

== ALLERGY: TEST FIRST ==
- Before using any plant on a wound or the skin, rub a little on the inside of the forearm and wait. Watch for redness, itch, swelling, or a rash.
- Aster and daisy family plants (like yarrow) set off common allergies, especially in people who react to ragweed or chamomile.
- If a remedy makes the skin more irritated, stop and wash it off.

== PREGNANCY, NURSING, AND CHILDREN ==
- Pregnant or nursing women should avoid internal remedies (willow bark, pine needle tea) entirely unless there is truly no choice.
- NEVER give willow bark, or any aspirin-family remedy, to a child or teenager with a fever or viral illness. It risks Reye's syndrome, a rare but deadly reaction. This is absolute.
- Never give honey to a baby under 1 year old. Risk of infant botulism.
- Children need smaller amounts and more caution across the board.

== DRUG INTERACTIONS ==
- Willow bark is the aspirin family. Avoid it with blood thinners, other pain or fever medicine, stomach ulcers, or an aspirin allergy.
- If you take any prescription medicine, assume a plant remedy could interact and be cautious.

== CLEAN FIRST, ALWAYS ==
- Wash your hands and the wound with the cleanest water you have before applying any poultice.
- A dirty poultice on a wound causes infection. When in doubt, a clean dressing and clean water beat any plant.

== DOSAGE: LESS IS SAFER ==
- Start weak, start small, use rarely. Many plant compounds are helpful in a trace and toxic in quantity.
- More is not better. If a remedy is not clearly helping, stop.

== WHEN IN DOUBT, DO NOTHING ==
- Doing nothing is almost always safer than a wrong remedy.
- Rest, clean water, a clean covered wound, warmth, and evacuation carry you further than any plant.
- A plant that might help is never worth mistaking for a plant that can kill.
