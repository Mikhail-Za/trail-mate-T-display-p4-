Prickly Pear and Aloe (burns and skin)

== READ FIRST ==
- These plants soothe and dress a burn. They do NOT replace the burn steps below. Do those first.
- Getting to medical care is ONE option, not the whole plan. If you can safely reach help, do. If you cannot, the field steps here are your plan. Work them well and keep at them.

== STEP 1: STOP AND COOL THE BURN ==
- Stop the source: smother flames (stop, drop, roll), move off hot metal, wash off chemicals, cut power for electrical.
- Strip hot or soaked clothing and ALL rings, watches, and tight items NOW, before swelling starts. Do not peel skin stuck to melted fabric; cut around it.
- Cool with clean, cool (not icy) running or poured water for 10 to 20 minutes. It still helps if you start within the first few hours.
- NO ice, NO snow. Ice shrinks blood flow, deepens the burn, and can cause frostbite and body-wide chilling.
- On a large burn, cool the burn but keep the rest of the body warm. Big burns lose heat fast and chill kills.

== STEP 2: SIZE IT UP ==
- Depth:
  - First-degree: red, dry, painful, no blisters (like bad sunburn). Heals in days.
  - Second-degree: red, wet, blistered, very painful. A real open wound.
  - Third-degree: leathery, waxy white, brown or charred, dry, often numb. Deep and dangerous.
- Size: your whole palm plus fingers is about 1 percent of your skin. Count palms.
- Danger flags (see HARD TRUTHS): face, neck, hands, feet, genitals, or over a joint; all the way around a limb, finger, or chest; larger than about 10 palms; any third-degree; or smoke or steam breathed in.

== STEP 3: CLEAN AND DRESS ==
- After cooling, gently rinse the burn with clean or disinfected water (boiled then cooled, or treated). Mild soap on the skin AROUND it is fine.
- Blisters: leave them INTACT. An unbroken blister is a sterile bandage. If one has already torn, trim away only the loose dead skin with a clean blade or scissors, then cover.
- Cover with the cleanest NON-STICK material you have. Change it once a day, and any time it is soaked or dirty. Look at the wound each change.
- NEVER put butter, oil, grease, lard, toothpaste, or ash on a burn. They trap heat and seed infection.

== DRESSING OPTIONS (best to fallback) ==
- Best: clean non-stick dressing with a thin layer of antibiotic ointment, if you carry one.
- Honey: if you carry honey, spread a thin layer directly on a cleaned second-degree burn and cover. Honey is antimicrobial and in studies healed partial-thickness burns as fast as or faster than hospital creams. A strong field choice when you have no antibiotics.
- Aloe inner gel: soothing, anti-inflammatory, good pain relief for first- and second-degree burns and sunburn. Reapply as it dries.
- Prickly pear inner gel and pulp: a moist, soothing cover used as a poultice for generations; the mucilage holds moisture over the wound.
- Fallback: any clean cloth cover is better than an open, exposed burn.

== ALOE (Aloe vera) ==
- Common houseplant; naturalized in frost-free areas: parts of the desert Southwest, southern California, the Gulf coast, and Florida.
- Use: snap off a thick leaf, scrape out the CLEAR inner gel, spread on the burn.
- Use ONLY the clear inner gel. The yellow sap (latex) just under the skin is a strong laxative and irritant. Rinse it off the gel before use.

== PRICKLY PEAR (Opuntia) ==
- Native across the desert Southwest, southern plains, and Southeast: flat, green, paddle-shaped pads set with spines.
- Use: cut a pad, singe or scrape off ALL the spines and the tiny hair-fine glochids, split the pad, and press the wet inner gel and pulp on the burn or scrape.
- GLOCHIDS: the little tufts of hair-fine spines lodge in skin and are miserable. Handle pads with a cloth and clear off every glochid before use.
- If glochids get in your skin: pull the big ones with tweezers, then lay tape (or a thin coat of glue or pine sap let to dry) over the area and peel it off to lift the rest. Repeat. Wash with clean water.

== LOOKALIKE: AGAVE (not aloe) ==
- Agave looks like a giant aloe but its sap is caustic. It carries oxalate crystals and saponins that cause a burning, sometimes bruise-like rash and eye damage.
- Aloe leaves are soft, fleshy, full of clear gel. Agave leaves are tough and fibrous with a hard, sharp end spine. Big, stiff, and fibrous means NOT aloe.

== IF YOU CANNOT REACH HELP ==
Manage the wound over days. This is the real work.
- Watch for infection: pain rising again after day 2 or 3, redness spreading past the burn edge, pus, bad smell, red streaks running up the limb, fever or chills.
- Fight infection: reopen the dressing, rinse pus away with clean or boiled-and-cooled water, trim loose dead tissue, apply honey if you have it, and change the dressing once or twice a day. Take any antibiotics you carry exactly per their label. Elevate the part. Honest limit: cleaning and honey slow infection, but a deep infection spreading into the blood (sepsis) can still kill, and there is no field cure for that.
- Fluids for a large burn: a burn over about a fifth of the body leaks huge fluid and can drop you into shock. If you have no IV, DRINK. Field rehydration mix: 1 quart or liter of clean water, about 6 level teaspoons sugar, and half a level teaspoon salt. Sip steadily and continuously. Oral fluids alone can carry a burn as large as about 40 percent of the body when nothing else exists. Target: keep passing pale urine regularly. Dark or scant urine means you are behind, so drink more.
- Pain: cooling early, keeping it covered, elevating, and holding the part still all reduce pain. Use any pain medicine you carry.
- Keep the dressing moist-covered, keep the person warm and resting, and protect the wound from dirt.

== HARD TRUTHS (NO FIELD CURE) ==
Be clear-eyed. These will not close in the field, and pretending otherwise gets people killed.
- Deep (third-degree) burns bigger than a few palms need surgery and skin grafts to close. In the field they will not heal over and are a major infection and death risk. Do everything above (clean, honey or cover, hydrate, prevent infection, control pain) and treat reaching help as the priority. No plant, gel, or remedy closes a deep burn.
- Breathed-in burns (smoke or steam, soot in mouth or nose, singed nose hair, hoarse or croaky voice, trouble breathing): the airway can swell shut over hours and there is no field fix. Keep the person upright and calm, in the coolest cleanest air you can, and get to help hardest for this one. Say it plainly: a closing airway with no equipment can be fatal.
- Burns all the way around a limb, finger, or the chest: as the dead skin swells it can choke off blood flow or breathing. Elevate and remove every tight thing. Cutting the tight burned skin to release it (escharotomy) is the ONLY relief, but it is a bloody surgical act of true last resort, done only if the part is going cold and dead or breathing is failing and no help can come. Not a routine step.
- Chemical burns: flush with large amounts of water for 20 minutes or more (longer for alkali). Electrical burns hide deep tissue and heart damage under small skin marks; assume worse than it looks.

== CAUTION ==
- The plant gels are for minor first-degree and small second-degree burns, sunburn, and skin irritation.
- Patch-test first; some people react to aloe or cactus.
- Do not pack plant pulp deep into an open, deep wound.

== SEASON ==
- Both plants are usable year-round within their range.
