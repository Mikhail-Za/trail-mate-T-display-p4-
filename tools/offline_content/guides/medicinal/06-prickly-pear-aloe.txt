Prickly Pear and Aloe (burns and skin)

== WHAT THEY DO ==
- The cool inner gel of a prickly pear pad or an aloe leaf soothes minor burns, sunburn, and dry or irritated skin.
- Topical use only.

== PRICKLY PEAR (Opuntia) ==
- Native across the desert Southwest, the southern plains, and the Southeast: flat, green, paddle-shaped pads set with spines.
- Use: carefully cut a pad, singe or scrape off ALL the spines and the tiny hair-like glochids, split the pad, and press the wet inner gel and pulp on the burn or scrape.
- GLOCHIDS WARNING: the little tufts of hair-fine spines lodge in skin and are miserable to remove. Handle the pad with a cloth and clear off every glochid before you use it.

== ALOE (Aloe vera) ==
- Not native to North America, but common as a houseplant and naturalized in frost-free areas: parts of the desert Southwest, southern California, the Gulf coast, and Florida.
- Use: snap off a thick leaf, scrape out the clear inner gel, and spread it on the burn or sunburn.
- Use ONLY the clear inner gel. The yellow sap just under the skin of the leaf is a strong laxative and irritant. Rinse it off the gel.

== LOOKALIKE: AGAVE (not aloe) ==
- Agave looks like a giant aloe, but its sap is caustic and irritating to skin and eyes.
- Aloe leaves are soft, fleshy, and full of clear gel. Agave leaves are tough and fibrous with a hard, sharp spine at the tip. If it is big, stiff, and fibrous, it is not aloe.

== CAUTION ==
- Topical only, for minor first-degree burns and skin irritation.
- Serious burns (blistering, large, deep, or on the face, hands, or joints) need real medical care. Cool with clean water and evacuate.
- Patch-test first. Some people react to aloe or to cactus.
- Do not apply to open, deep wounds.

== SEASON ==
- Both are usable year-round within their range.
