Conifer Resin (Pine, Spruce, Fir Pitch): wound seal

== WHAT IT DOES ==
- The sticky sap, or pitch, of pines, spruces, and firs is naturally antiseptic and sticky.
- On the skin it can seal and protect a small, cleaned cut, crack, or blister, and help keep dirt out. Think of it as a rough bandage in a pinch.
- Old folk use for small wounds and cracked skin. Topical only.

== SOURCE AND ID ==
- Use any true conifer (pine, spruce, or fir) that oozes sticky resin at a bark wound.
- Take hardened amber pitch or the soft sticky pitch from a wound or a fallen limb. Do not gouge live trees needlessly.
- Do NOT collect from YEW or any tree you cannot name. See the Pine entry for the deadly yew warning.

== HOW TO USE ==
- Clean the wound and control bleeding first.
- Warm a small amount of pitch, between clean fingers or gently near a fire, until it softens.
- Warm it gently and keep it away from open flame; pitch is flammable.
- Smear a thin layer over the CLOSED, cleaned minor wound or skin crack, then cover with a clean dressing.
- It also helps seal cracked lips, split fingertips, and heels. Spruce and fir pitch is often softer and easier to spread than pine.
- Some warm the pitch with a little animal fat or oil to make a salve that spreads more easily.

== CAUTION ==
- Topical only, for small clean wounds. Never seal a deep, dirty, or infected wound. Trapping bacteria inside is dangerous. Deep or dirty wounds stay open (see the first-aid section) and need a professional.
- Resin can irritate skin or cause an allergic rash in some people. Patch-test a small spot first.
- It is very sticky and hard to remove; oil or fat loosens it.
- Do not use it on burns.

== SEASON ==
- Available year-round. Pitch flows more freely in warm weather.
