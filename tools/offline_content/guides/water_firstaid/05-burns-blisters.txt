Burns and Blisters

Get professional care for: any burn larger than the casualty's palm, any full thickness burn, burns to the face, hands, feet, genitals, or all the way around a limb, and any electrical or chemical burn. A burn near the airway (singed nose hairs, soot around the mouth, hoarse voice, coughing) can swell the throat shut. That is an emergency: evacuate immediately.

== RECOGNIZING BURN SEVERITY ==
- Superficial (first degree): red, dry, painful. Like sunburn. Heals on its own.
- Partial thickness (second degree): blisters, wet shiny surface, very painful. Small ones can be managed in the field; large ones need care.
- Full thickness (third degree): white, leathery, or charred. May be painless in the center because nerves are destroyed. Always needs professional care.

== FIRST AID: COOL WATER ==
Stop the burning first. Put out flames, move away from the heat source, and remove rings, watches, and clothing near the burn before swelling starts. Do not peel off cloth stuck to the skin; cool over it and leave it.

- Cool the burn with cool, clean running water for 20 minutes. This is worth doing up to 3 hours after the burn.
- Never use ice or snow directly on a burn. Extreme cold kills more tissue and deepens the injury.
- With large burns, cooling can chill the whole person. Cool the burn, keep the rest of the casualty warm and dry, and watch for shivering.

== COVERING BURNS ==
- After cooling, cover loosely with a non-stick dressing, clean plastic wrap, or the cleanest cloth available.
- Do not pop burn blisters. They are a sterile natural dressing.
- Do not apply butter, grease, or toothpaste. Aloe or plain moisturizer is fine on superficial burns only.
- Burns leak fluid. Give the casualty plenty of water, and pain medicine such as ibuprofen if available and safe for them.

== BLISTER PREVENTION: ACT AT THE HOT SPOT ==
A blister announces itself as a hot spot: a point of rubbing warmth or sting. Stop walking the moment you feel one. Ten minutes now saves a trip-ending wound later.

- Cover the hot spot with tape or moleskin before a blister forms.
- Smooth sock wrinkles, shake out grit, dry your feet, and re-lace the boot.
- Damp feet blister fast. Change or dry socks at breaks.

== DRAIN OR PROTECT ==
An intact blister roof is the best dressing there is. Protect it when you can.

- Small blister, not on a weight-bearing spot: pad it and leave it alone.
- Large, painful blister that you must walk on: draining it is reasonable. Clean the skin, heat a needle in flame until it glows, let it cool, puncture at the blister's edge, and press the fluid out. Leave the roof in place.
- Never cut away or peel off the roof.
- Never drain blood-filled blisters, or blisters from burns or frostbite. Cover and protect those.

== MOLESKIN AND TAPE TECHNIQUE ==
- Cut a doughnut of moleskin with the hole slightly larger than the blister. Stick it around, not on, the blister. Stack a second doughnut if it still takes pressure.
- Cover the hole with tape so the dressing does not stick to the roof.
- Round the corners of every tape piece so it does not peel. Apply to clean, dry skin.
- Check daily. Spreading redness, pus, red streaks, or fever mean infection: clean it, dress it, and get professional care.
