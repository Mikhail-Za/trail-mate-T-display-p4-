Making Water Safe

Most backcountry water in North America can carry Giardia, Cryptosporidium, and bacteria; near people and livestock, viruses too. You usually cannot see, smell, or taste any of them. But you are almost never helpless: with a pot, a few tablets, a clear bottle and sun, or just cloth and time, you can make bad water drinkable. Pick the best method you can do right now and do it. Do not wait to be rescued to drink.

== DO THIS FIRST ==
Work down this list to the best option you can manage:

- Can you make fire and have a pot? BOIL it. Most reliable.
- Have chlorine dioxide tablets, iodine, or plain bleach? Treat it.
- Have a clear plastic bottle and sun? Use SOLAR (SODIS).
- Have a 0.2 micron filter? Filter it (add a step for viruses near people).
- Cloudy or muddy? Clear it FIRST, then use one of the above.
- None of these and going downhill from thirst? See the honest last resort at the end.

Whatever you choose, keep treating every batch. One clean drink does not protect the next.

== BOILING: MOST RELIABLE ==
Boiling kills protozoa, bacteria, and viruses. It works at any altitude.

- The moment water reaches a full rolling boil, big bubbles breaking the whole surface, it is already safe. The heat, not the minutes, does the killing.
- CDC says hold the boil 1 minute for a simple safety margin. Above about 6,500 feet, hold 3 minutes.
- If fuel is scarce, do not waste it: reaching a hard boil is enough. Pull it as soon as it boils rather than burning your last fuel on a long boil.
- Let it cool. Pouring between containers improves the flat taste.

Boiling does not remove sediment, salt, or chemicals.

== SOLAR DISINFECTION (SODIS): NO FUEL, NO GEAR ==
Sunlight alone disinfects clear water. WHO-recognized. Slow but it costs you nothing but time.

- Use a CLEAR plastic (PET) drink bottle, 2 liters or smaller. Not glass, not colored, not scratched or cracked.
- Water must be clear enough to read text through the bottle. Settle or filter cloudy water first (see below).
- Fill it almost full, cap it, shake 20 seconds to add oxygen, then top off.
- Lay it on its SIDE in direct sun. A bright rock, sand, or metal (foil, a pack frame) under it reflects more light in.
- Leave it 6 full hours of bright sun. Half-cloudy: still 6 hours. Fully overcast: 2 days.
- Kills bacteria, viruses, and Giardia. It does NOT
  reliably kill Cryptosporidium: for that use boiling, a
  0.2 micron filter, or chlorine dioxide.

Does not remove chemicals, salt, or algal toxins.

== CHEMICAL TREATMENT ==
Carry these light; they need no fire.

- Chlorine dioxide tablets or drops: follow the label. Typical wait is 30 minutes for bacteria, viruses, and Giardia, but 4 hours to kill Cryptosporidium.
- Iodine tablets or drops: wait 30 minutes in clear, warm water. Iodine does NOT reliably kill Cryptosporidium. Avoid iodine if pregnant or with thyroid disease, and do not use it more than about 1 month straight.
- Plain household bleach (improvised): use only unscented 5 to 9 percent sodium hypochlorite, no additives or scent.
  - Clear water: 2 drops per liter (about 8 drops per gallon). Mix, wait 30 minutes.
  - Cloudy or very cold water: double it to 4 drops per liter (16 per gallon).
  - After the wait it should smell faintly of chlorine. No smell? Add one more dose and wait 15 minutes. Still no smell after that: discard, the water is too dirty for bleach alone.
  - Bleach, like all chlorine, does NOT reliably kill Cryptosporidium.
- Cold water caveat: below about 40 degrees F, all chemicals work slowly. Double the contact time, or warm the bottle inside your jacket first.
- Cloudy water: clear it first, then double the dose or the time per the label.
- After the wait, loosen the cap and slosh treated water over the threads and lid so untreated drops do not reinfect your mouth.

== FILTERS ==
- Filters (0.2 micron class) remove protozoa (including Cryptosporidium) and bacteria, but NOT viruses. In remote North American backcountry the virus risk is low, so a good filter is usually adequate.
- Near towns, farms, camps, and heavily traveled water, add a virus step: after filtering, also boil, use SODIS, or add chlorine dioxide.
- Protect filters from freezing. A filter that has frozen may be cracked inside and passing pathogens while looking fine. After a hard freeze, treat it as unreliable.

== UV PENS ==
- UV pens work only in clear water. Stir for the full cycle per the instructions.
- Useless in cloudy water, and the device can fail or run out of batteries. Carry a backup method.

== CLEAR CLOUDY WATER FIRST ==
Mud and debris shield pathogens and eat up chemicals and UV. Clarifying is a prep step, NOT treatment. Always follow it with boiling, SODIS, or chemicals.

- Settle: let dirty water stand 1 hour, or overnight for a mud slurry, then pour or siphon off the clear top layer.
- Cloth prefilter: pour through a bandana folded several times, a tightly woven shirt, or a sock packed in layers: grass on top, crushed charcoal from a dead fire (charcoal, not ash) in the middle, sand at the bottom. This clears grit and improves taste only.
- Coagulate (if you carry alum): stir in a pinch (about 1/8 teaspoon per 4 liters), swirl briskly 1 minute, then gently 5 minutes, settle 30 minutes, pour off through cloth. This drops out most particles and many microbes but is still NOT safe to drink until treated.

== SALT OR BRACKISH WATER: DISTILL IT ==
Boiling, filters, and chemicals do NOT remove salt. Drinking seawater or salty water pulls water OUT of you and speeds death. Distillation is the only field fix, and it also removes most chemicals, heavy metals, and all germs.

- Stovetop still: boil water in a pot with a tilted lid or a cup rigged to catch steam. The steam that condenses on a cool surface and drips into a clean container is pure water. Collect the drips; discard the salty pot water.
- Solar still: dig a pit, set a clean cup in the center, add wet vegetation or salty water around it, cover with a clear plastic sheet weighted with a stone over the cup. Sun evaporates water; it condenses on the sheet and drips into the cup. Yields little (often under a cup a day) but needs no fuel.

Honest caveat: distillation does NOT remove fuel, solvents, or other vapors that boil at or below water. If it smells of fuel or chemicals, distilling will not make it safe. Find other water.

== WHAT NO FIELD METHOD CAN FIX ==
Heat, chemicals, UV, and sun kill living organisms only. None of them, and no filter you can carry, remove:

- Chemical contamination: mine runoff, farm runoff, fuel, solvents, industrial pollution.
- Algal toxins from scummy, pea-soup, or blue-green blooms. Boiling can make these WORSE by releasing more toxin from the cells.
- Salt or alkali (distill for salt only, see above).
- Radioactive fallout.

The hard truth: there is no reliable field cure for chemical or algal-toxin contamination. Do not drink it, do not try to filter or boil it clean, and do not trust a folk remedy. Move on. Better sources: a spring, a fast clear side stream above the pollution, rainfall caught on a clean sheet, clean snow melted (never eat it frozen, it costs you heat), or dew wiped from plants at dawn.

== IF YOU GET SICK FROM WATER ==
Waterborne illness in North America usually brings diarrhea days to weeks later, not on the spot. The thing that actually kills fast is the DEHYDRATION from that diarrhea, and you can manage that yourself.

- Keep drinking treated water. Do NOT stop drinking because of diarrhea; that is what harms you.
- Make oral rehydration solution: 1 liter of treated water, 6 level teaspoons of sugar, and 1/2 level teaspoon of salt. Stir and sip steadily all day. This is the single most important thing you can do.
- Eat bland starchy food (crackers, rice, plain carbs) if you can keep it down.
- Rest, stay warm, and keep treating all water so you do not reinfect yourself.
- Most healthy adults recover on their own with fluids.
- If you carry antibiotics you were prescribed for travel and the diarrhea is bloody or comes with high fever, that is when they are for: azithromycin 500 mg once a day for 3 days is a standard course. Use only medicine you carry and were told how to take. Do not use antibiotics for ordinary watery diarrhea.
- Evacuate if you can safely reach help AND you see danger signs fluids are not turning around: no urine, sunken eyes, confusion, or a fast weak pulse.

== DRINKING UNTREATED WATER: THE HONEST LAST RESORT ==
Dehydration can kill you in days. Most North American waterborne illness causes diarrhea days to weeks later and is survivable. If you have no way to treat water and you are sliding into dehydration (dark urine, headache, dizziness, confusion), drink the cleanest water you can find rather than continuing to dry out.

- Choose a spring, fast clear stream, or rain catch over still or stagnant water.
- Even now, avoid anything with a chemical smell, an oily sheen, or algal scum. Those can hurt you fast; germs are the slower risk.
- Prefilter through cloth if you can.
- Note where and when you drank it, and tell medical providers later so they can watch for illness.

Do not let fear of germs stop you from drinking when serious dehydration threatens. Stay hydrated, stay sharp, get out.
