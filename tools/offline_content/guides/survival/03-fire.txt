Fire

Fire gives heat, light, signaling, and morale. It also escapes. An escaped fire can destroy your shelter, your gear, and you, and in dry, windy conditions one ember can start a wildfire. Respect it before you strike it.

== SAFETY FIRST: LEAVE NO ESCAPE RISK ==

- Never build a fire you cannot control and put out. In high wind over dry grass or brush, do not light one at all; rely on shelter and insulation instead.
- Clear a circle down to bare mineral soil, at least 3 feet across, wider in wind.
- Keep the fire small. A small, tended fire warms better and escapes less than a bonfire.
- Stay clear of overhanging branches, dry grass, deep duff, and root systems; fire can smolder underground along roots for days.
- Keep water, snow, or loose soil at hand before lighting.
- Never leave it unattended. Before you abandon a site, drown it, stir it, and feel it cold with your hand.

== TINDER, KINDLING, FUEL ==

Fires fail from skipped steps. Gather everything before you strike:

- Tinder catches a spark or small flame: dry grass, shredded inner bark, birch bark, fatwood shavings, dry pine needles, cotton or gauze from a first aid kit. A double handful, bone dry.
- Kindling is dry twigs from pencil-lead up to finger thickness. Two big handfuls, sorted by size.
- Fuel is wrist thick and up. Collect at least twice what you think you need before lighting.

Progress in order. Add each size only when the previous stage is burning strongly.

== FEATHER STICKS ==

When everything small is wet, make your own kindling. Take a dry split stick and shave long, thin curls down its length, leaving them attached in a cluster. The thin curls catch flame like tinder while the stick body burns like kindling. Three or four feather sticks can start a fire in poor conditions.

== LIGHTER AND FERRO ROD IN WIND AND WET ==

- Prepare the whole fire lay first, then light. Do not light tinder and then start gathering.
- Shield the flame with your body, a rock, or a shallow pit. Light from the upwind side so the flame blows into the tinder, not away from it.
- Warm a cold lighter in your hand or an inner pocket first; cold butane lighters fail.
- With a ferro rod, hold the rod tip low, touching the tinder, brace the scraper, and pull the rod back against it instead of pushing sparks forward. This keeps the spark shower on target without scattering your tinder.

== FIRE IN RAIN ==

Wet ground and wet wood do not mean no fire:

- Take standing deadwood: dead branches still attached to trees or dead trunks still upright. Never use wood lying flat on wet ground.
- Split it. Even soaked wood is usually dry at the core. Baton thick pieces apart with a knife and a wooden club, or break and shave down to the dry wood inside.
- Build on a raised platform of bark or split wood so the base is not sitting in mud.
- Shelter the fire lay while starting it: a leaning slab of bark, your body, or a rock face, kept well away from your own shelter.

== KEEPING IT OVERNIGHT ==

- Bank the fire before sleeping: let it burn to a strong coal bed, then lay two or three thick logs over the coals and partly cover with ash to slow the burn.
- Stack extra fuel within arm's reach so you can feed it without leaving your insulation.
- Expect to wake and tend it. Face your shelter opening toward the fire at a safe distance, with no bedding material between you and it.
