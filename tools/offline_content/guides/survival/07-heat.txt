Heat Illness

Heat kills by outpacing the body's cooling. The spectrum runs from heat cramps to heat exhaustion to heat stroke, and the line between the last two is the most important thing on this page.

== HEAT EXHAUSTION VS HEAT STROKE ==

Heat exhaustion: heavy sweating, pale and clammy skin, weakness, headache, nausea, dizziness, cramps. The person feels terrible but is thinking normally.

Heat stroke: the brain is overheating. Altered mental status is the red flag: confusion, combativeness, slurred speech, stumbling, seizures, or collapse. Skin is hot and flushed and may be dry or still sweaty. Body temperature is dangerously high.

The difference that matters: heat exhaustion is treated with rest, shade, and fluids. Heat stroke is a life threatening emergency measured in minutes.

== HEAT STROKE: EMERGENCY RESPONSE ==

- Cool first, evacuate second. Aggressive cooling right now is what saves the brain.
- Move them to shade, strip excess clothing, and soak them with any water available. Fan hard; evaporation is your best tool in dry air.
- Cool the neck, armpits, and groin with wet cloth. If a stream or lake is at hand and you can keep the airway safe, cool the whole body in it.
- Do not give fluids to anyone who is confused or not fully awake; they can choke.
- Call for rescue immediately. Even if they seem to recover, they need medical care.

== HEAT EXHAUSTION: TREATMENT ==

- Stop all work. Shade, rest, loosen clothing, wet the skin, and fan.
- Give small, steady sips of water with electrolytes, or water plus salty food. Recovery takes hours, not minutes. Do not resume hard effort the same day if you can avoid it.
- Watch closely. Any confusion or worsening means treat it as heat stroke.

== HYDRATION AND ELECTROLYTES ==

- Drink steadily during hot effort, roughly half a liter to a liter per hour, and let urine guide you: pale yellow is good, dark means drink more.
- Water alone is not enough over long, sweaty days. Replace salt with electrolyte mix, salty snacks, or regular food.
- Warning: drinking large amounts of plain water without salt or food can cause hyponatremia, low blood sodium, which also causes confusion and can be fatal, and it mimics heat illness. If someone is bloated and nauseated and has been drinking heavily without eating, more plain water is the wrong medicine. Give salty food and rest, and get help if their mental status changes.

== WORK-REST CYCLES ==

- In serious heat, work in short bouts with shaded rests. In the worst conditions, favor 15 to 20 minutes of effort with equal or longer rest, and adjust honestly to conditions and how people look.
- Slow your pace before you feel bad. In heat, the feeling of "fine" lags behind reality.
- Watch each other. The person overheating is usually the last one to notice.

== SHADE IMPROVISATION ==

- Any shade cuts heat load sharply. Rig a tarp, emergency blanket, or spare clothing high enough for air to flow underneath. A double layer with an air gap between is cooler than a single layer.
- Get off the ground. Desert ground can be 30 to 50 degrees F hotter than the air. Sit on a pack or a brush pile in the shade.
- In open desert, the shadow of a boulder, a cutbank, or a vehicle can be the difference that gets you to evening.

== DESERT TRAVEL TIMING ==

- Travel at dawn, at dusk, or at night when it is safe to do so. Hole up in shade through midday, roughly 10 a.m. to 4 p.m.
- If stranded with a vehicle, stay with it. It is shade, shelter, and a signal that searchers can see. Do not sit inside a closed, hot car.
- Ration sweat, not water: move slowly, rest often, and drink what you carry. People have been found dead with water still in their packs.
