Food, Rationing, and Morale

== PRIORITIES FIRST ==
- In short-term survival, food sits far down the list. A healthy person can go weeks without food, but cold can kill in hours and dehydration in days.
- Spend your energy on shelter, warmth, water, and being findable. Hunger is loud, but on this timescale it is discomfort, not danger.

== RATION FROM THE START ==
- The moment you realize you are stuck, inventory every scrap of food, pool it, and put one person in charge of it.
- Do not eat normally on day one and starve later. Cut to a third or half of normal intake immediately and stretch it across the days you might wait.
- Small regular portions beat rare feasts. Something in the stomach at set times steadies mood and marks the passage of time.
- Digestion uses water. If water is critically short, eat very little until you have solved water.
- Save the densest food, fats and nuts, for the coldest nights.

== WHY FORAGING IS USUALLY A BAD BET ==
- Wild plant calories are sparse, and a misidentification can cause vomiting and diarrhea, which cost you water and heat you cannot spare.
- Hours spent foraging are hours not spent on shelter, signals, and rest, usually traded for a handful of calories.
- Follow the foraging rules elsewhere in this Field Guide, and the first rule above all: if you are not completely certain of an identification, it is not food.

== HUNGER VS REAL STARVATION ==
- Normal hunger: stomach growling, constant food thoughts, irritability, mild weakness. It peaks around days two to three and then often fades. This is discomfort, not damage.
- Real starvation takes weeks: pronounced weakness, visible muscle wasting, dizziness on standing, apathy, feeling cold all the time, slowed thinking.
- Do not make risky moves, like a dangerous descent or eating a mystery plant, to fix mere hunger discomfort.

== WHEN FOOD ACTUALLY MATTERS ==
- Cold environments change the math. Your body burns food to make heat, especially overnight, and shivering through a cold night on an empty stomach drains your core.
- In cold conditions keep some intake going every day, weight your ration toward evening, and eat something before sleep.
- Medical conditions come first: diabetics and others with condition-driven needs get rationed around before anyone else.

== KEEP BUSY, KEEP MORALE ==
- Morale is a survival resource. Idle waiting breeds fear; useful work steadies people.
- Set routines: improve the shelter each morning, gather firewood before dusk, signal at scheduled times, make scheduled check-ins on this device instead of draining the battery with constant use, tidy the camp.
- Give every person a job, including children: sorting wood, watching for searchers, keeping the whistle handy, tracking the days.
- Keep small rituals: a warm drink, a story at dusk, a mark on a stick for each day. Visible, fair rationing is itself a morale tool; it prevents the fights that split groups.
- Say the plan out loud daily: what we do today, and what would change it.

== THE BOTTOM LINE ==
- Water, warmth, shelter, signals, then food.
- Ration early, forage almost never, keep hands busy, and let hunger stay background noise while you work on being found.
