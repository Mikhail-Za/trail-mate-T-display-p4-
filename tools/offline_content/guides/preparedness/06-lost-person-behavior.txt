If Someone in Your Group Is Missing

== STOP AND MARK ==
- The moment you notice someone missing, STOP the whole group. Do not let people scatter; a second lost person doubles the problem.
- Establish the point last seen: where and WHEN someone actually saw the missing person, not where you assume they went. Write down the time.
- Mark that point so it cannot be lost: note it on the Map app, and leave a physical marker (bright gear, cairn, note).
- On this device, check the Team page for the missing member's last reported position, and send a LoRa chat message immediately. They may be in range and merely off-trail.

== TRY THE EASY WINS FIRST ==
- Call and whistle from the point last seen, then LISTEN. Sound finds most misplaced people quickly.
- Whistle protocol: three blasts means "I need help." Searchers blow ONE long blast, then stay silent and listen for at least 30 seconds. Wind and running water hide replies; cup your ears and turn slowly.
- Check the obvious spots: the last junction, the last rest stop, water sources, viewpoints, and the direction of the trailhead.

== HASTY SEARCH, NOT A WANDER ==
- Search in pairs, never alone, each pair with a hard turnaround time and a route.
- Trail sweeps: pairs move out along each trail and obvious route from the point last seen, calling and whistling, going a fixed time out (for example 15 minutes) and back, checking junctions and drainage entrances.
- Sound sweeps: spread searchers in a line within earshot of one another, advance slowly, whistle, stop, listen, advance again.
- Everyone returns to the marked point at the agreed time, found or not. A search that loses its searchers has failed twice.

== SEARCH YOURSELVES OR GO FOR HELP? ==
- Keep searching yourselves while: the person was seen recently, the area is small, weather is mild, daylight remains, and you have people to spare.
- Go for help NOW when: the missing person is a child, elderly, injured, ill, or unequipped; night or a storm is coming; the terrain holds cliffs or fast water; or an hour of hasty searching has found nothing.
- Going for help is not giving up. Professional searchers are good at this, and the earlier they start, the smaller the search area.
- If you can, leave one person at the point last seen. Lost people often wander back to it.

== NOTES AND MARKERS ==
- Leave notes at the point last seen and at nearby junctions: who is missing, the time, where the group went, and an instruction ("Stay here. We return by 4 pm.").
- Weight notes with rocks or pin them at eye level. Make markers unmissable and point them in your direction of travel.

== WHAT SEARCHERS NEED TO KNOW ==
- Write it down before stress erases it: point last seen and time, full clothing description with colors, footwear and tread if known, physical and mental condition, medical issues, direction of travel, gear carried, and experience level.
- A phone photo of the person from that day is gold. Exact times matter more than you think.

== WHY LOST PEOPLE MOVE ==
- Lost people rarely stay put. Embarrassment and "it must be just ahead" keep them walking.
- They drift DOWNHILL and follow paths of least resistance: trails, gullies, drainages, open timber, roads.
- They follow water downstream, hit barriers like rivers, cliffs, and fences, then travel along them.
- So check downhill first, check the drainage, check linear features. And brief your group beforehand: if you are lost, STOP, stay put, and answer whistles.
