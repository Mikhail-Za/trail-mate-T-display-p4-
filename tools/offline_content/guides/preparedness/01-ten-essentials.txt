The Ten Essentials, Modernized

== A SYSTEM, NOT A SHOPPING LIST ==
- The ten essentials are ten problems you must be able to answer: where am I, sun, cold, dark, injury, fire, broken gear, hunger, thirst, and an unplanned night out.
- Carry them on every trip, including short ones. Most rescues begin as short trips.

== 1. NAVIGATION ==
- Carry this device with offline maps loaded for your route, plus a paper map and a baseplate compass.
- Electronics fail from cold, water, drops, and dead batteries. Paper and a compass do not. Learn to use both before you need them.
- Check your position at every junction and major terrain change, not just when you feel unsure.

== 2. SUN PROTECTION ==
- Sunglasses, sunscreen, lip balm, and a brimmed hat. Snow, water, and altitude multiply exposure.

== 3. INSULATION ==
- Carry one warm layer more than the forecast demands, plus hat and gloves in shoulder seasons.
- Avoid cotton next to skin; wet cotton drains heat. Wool and synthetics insulate even when damp.

== 4. ILLUMINATION ==
- Headlamp with spare batteries, tested before the trip.
- The Flashlight app on this device works in a pinch, but it spends the same battery you need for maps and chat. Treat it as a backup and a night signal, not your main light.

== 5. FIRST AID ==
- A small kit you actually know how to use: blister care, wound dressings, tape, gauze, pain reliever, personal medications, gloves.
- Tailor it to trip length and group size.

== 6. FIRE ==
- Two ways to make flame: a lighter plus stormproof matches or a ferro rod.
- Carry ready tinder (cotton balls with petroleum jelly, commercial cubes). Wet forests do not hand you dry tinder.

== 7. REPAIR KIT AND TOOLS ==
- Knife or multitool, duct tape, a few feet of cordage, safety pins, gear patches.
- Enough to fix a pack strap, a pole, a shoe, or a shelter in the field.

== 8. NUTRITION ==
- One extra day of food that needs no cooking: bars, nuts, jerky.

== 9. HYDRATION ==
- Water on your body, plus the means to treat more: filter, tablets, or the knowledge to boil.
- Know the water sources along your route on the map before you start.

== 10. SHELTER ==
- Something between you and the ground and weather, even on day trips: an emergency blanket or bivy, or a small tarp.
- The goal is surviving one unplanned night, not comfort.

== TRIP PLAN DISCIPLINE ==
- Before you leave, tell a reliable person: where you are going, your exact route, who is with you, your vehicle and where it is parked, when you will return, and when to call for help if you have not checked in.
- Put it in writing. A text message is fine. Verbal plans get misremembered.
- Give a hard trigger time: "If you have not heard from me by 9 pm, call search and rescue." People act on trigger times, not on vague worry.
- Stick to the stated route. A perfect trip plan is useless if you improvise a new drainage without telling anyone.
- Close the loop: check in the moment you are out, so nobody launches a search for a hiker who is already home eating dinner.
