Using This Device When Lost or in Trouble

== FIRST, STOP ==
- Stop moving. Sit down, breathe, drink some water, and think. Panicked walking burns energy and makes you harder to find.
- Then work through the tools below, in order, while you are calm.

== YOUR LAST-KNOWN POSITION ==
- Open the Map app. With a GPS fix, you see your live position on the offline map.
- If GPS loses its fix, the map keeps your last-known position as a grey ring marker. That ring is where the device last knew you were.
- You cannot be far from that ring. Use it with the map to take a bearing back toward trails, ridgelines, or water you can identify.
- The marker survives after the fix is gone, so check the map before you wander.

== MESSAGE YOUR GROUP EARLY ==
- LoRa chat reaches your group miles out with no cell service. Terrain matters: ridgetops and open ground carry far, deep drainages cut range.
- Send your position and situation EARLY, while your battery is strong and your position is fresh. A short message beats a perfect one: "Off trail NE of the lake. Ankle injured. Staying put."
- If a message does not get through, gain a little elevation or open ground and try again.
- Once you have told the group you are staying put, stay put.

== THE TEAM PAGE ==
- The Team page shows the positions of your group members.
- If you are lost, it tells you where your people are. If you are the searcher, it shows where the missing member's device last reported.
- Compare Team page positions against the map before anyone moves.

== NIGHT SIGNALING ==
- The Flashlight app includes an SOS strobe. At night a strobe carries far beyond voice range, especially from a ridge, clearing, or shoreline.
- Aim it toward where help is likely: the trail, the road, your group's last position.
- Run it in bursts at intervals rather than continuously, and listen between bursts.

== BATTERY DISCIPLINE ==
- Battery is your lifeline. Turn screen brightness down as far as you can still read.
- Agree on a check-in schedule with your group, for example on the hour, instead of constant chat and a constantly lit screen.
- Between check-ins, leave the device alone. Send positions while power is good; a dead device shows nothing on anyone's Team page.
- Cold drains batteries fast. In cold weather, keep the device inside your jacket, close to your body, between uses.

== THIS GUIDE NEEDS NOTHING ==
- This Field Guide, your loaded offline maps, and the Translate phrasebook all work with zero connectivity. Nothing on this device needs the internet to do its job.
- Know the limit: this device does not call rescue services. It talks to your group. Your written trip plan and the person you left it with are still your link to organized rescue.

== PRACTICE BEFORE YOU NEED IT ==
- At the trailhead, confirm every group member can see the others on the Team page and can send a chat message.
- Agree on the check-in schedule and on what a missed check-in means, before anyone is tired, cold, or scared.
