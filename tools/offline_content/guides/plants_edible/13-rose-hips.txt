Rose Hips (wild Rosa species)

== WHERE ==
- Wild roses grow at forest edges, in old fields, on dunes, riverbanks, and roadsides across most of North America.
- Flowers late spring to summer; hips ripen red-orange in late summer and fall, sweeten after frost, and often hang on into winter.

== IDENTIFY ==
- Growth: a shrub or thicket of arching canes armed with thorns or dense prickles.
- Leaves: pinnately compound, usually 5 to 9 oval, toothed leaflets, with a pair of small wing-like flaps (stipules) where the leaf stalk meets the cane.
- Flowers: the classic wild rose: five broad petals, pink or white, around a boss of many yellow stamens; usually fragrant.
- Hips: round to egg-shaped fruits, orange-red when ripe, each carrying the dried whiskery remains of the sepals at its far end. Inside is pale firm flesh packed around hairy seeds.

== LOOKALIKES ==
- Demand the combination: thorny cane + compound rose leaves + whiskery sepal tuft on the fruit.
- Yew arils: DEADLY seed inside a soft red cup, open at one end, growing on an EVERGREEN with flat single needles. Nothing about it matches a rose cane. Rule: a red fruit on an evergreen is never a rose hip.
- Holly, bittersweet, and ornamental red berries: no sepal tuft, no thorny compound-leaved cane. Treat all of them as toxic.
- Hawthorn: a rose-family tree with red fruits; its flesh is eaten by many, but its seeds contain cyanide-forming compounds and it is a different plant. Do not substitute it for this entry.

== EDIBLE PARTS AND PREPARATION ==
- Hip flesh: nibble the shell off the outside, avoiding the hairy seed mass; or halve the hips, scrape out every seed and hair, and eat or dry the flesh.
- Tea: simmer whole hips 10 to 15 minutes, then STRAIN through tight cloth. Very high in vitamin C.
- Petals (in season) are edible; trim away the bitter white base.

== SEASON ==
- Flowers: late spring into summer; the petals are edible in season.
- Hips: ripen red-orange in late summer and fall, sweeten and soften after the first frosts, and often hang on the cane into winter as a cold-season vitamin source.

== CAUTION ==
- The seed hairs are a genuine irritant (the classic itching powder) and can inflame mouth, throat, and gut. Always deseed thoroughly or strain the tea through cloth.
- Skip hips along sprayed road edges and anywhere invasive-rose control is underway.
