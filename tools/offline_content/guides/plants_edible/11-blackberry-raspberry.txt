Blackberry and Raspberry (Rubus species)

== WHERE ==
- Sunny edges, old fields, clearings, fencerows, roadsides, and burn scars across most of North America.
- Flowers late spring; fruit ripens midsummer to late summer depending on species and elevation.

== IDENTIFY ==
- Growth: brambles; long arching canes armed with thorns or dense prickles. Raspberry canes often carry a whitish waxy bloom.
- Leaves: compound, usually 3 to 5 toothed leaflets, frequently pale to silvery underneath (strikingly white under raspberry leaves).
- Flowers: white to pink, five petals, with the open rose-family look.
- Fruit: a true AGGREGATE: many small juicy beads (drupelets) clustered into one berry, each bead holding one tiny seed.
- Pick test: a ripe raspberry pulls free as a hollow cap, leaving a pale core on the plant; a blackberry comes off with its solid core inside.

== LOOKALIKES ==
- None dangerous when the fruit is a genuine aggregate of drupelets on a thorny bramble. No aggregate berry of this form in North America is known to be dangerously toxic; this is the safest wild berry class, which is why it anchors this guide.
- The protection lives entirely in the FORM. A single smooth berry (nightshade, pokeweed, honeysuckle, buckthorn) gets no cover from this rule, whatever its color. Count the beads: many beads fused into one berry, or walk away.
- Mulberry: an aggregate-like fruit, but on a tree with simple leaves; also edible when fully ripe, though it is a different plant from these brambles.
- Unripe red blackberries are just sour and astringent, not dangerous.

== EDIBLE PARTS AND PREPARATION ==
- Ripe fruit raw: fully colored for its type, deep black for blackberries, red to deep purple-black for raspberries, releasing easily from the plant and tasting sweet.
- Fruit also dries well or cooks into anything.
- Leaves for tea: use them either fresh or fully dried; skip half-wilted leaves, which are traditionally avoided.

== CAUTION ==
- Eat ripe fruit only; unripe fruit is harmlessly unpleasant but not worth it.
- Thorns shred hands and forearms; harvest deliberately.
- Wash fruit from dusty roadsides. Berries are morale and vitamins, not meaningful calories.
