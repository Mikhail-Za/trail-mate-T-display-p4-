Chickweed (Stellaria media)

== WHERE ==
- Cool, moist, disturbed ground: gardens, yard edges, woodland margins, and shaded campsites across most of North America.
- A cool-season plant: lush in early spring and fall, and through mild winters; it fades in summer heat.

== IDENTIFY ==
- Growth: low, sprawling, tangled mats of weak round stems.
- The signature: a single line of fine hairs runs up ONE side of the stem, switching sides at each pair of leaves. Roll the stem in good light to see it.
- Leaves: opposite pairs, small, oval with a pointed tip, smooth edges.
- Flowers: tiny and white, five petals each split so deeply they look like ten.
- Stretch test: pull a stem gently apart; the outer skin breaks while a thin elastic inner cord stays connected.
- Sap: clear or none. Chickweed NEVER has milky sap.

== LOOKALIKES ==
- Spotted spurge: TOXIC and also mat-forming. It bleeds MILKY sap when broken and often shows a reddish spot on each leaf. Any milky sap means drop the plant.
- Scarlet pimpernel: mildly toxic and superficially similar, but its flowers are salmon-orange (occasionally blue), its stems are squarish, and it has no single line of stem hairs.
- Mouse-ear chickweed: fuzzy all over rather than one hair line; commonly eaten cooked, but hold every plant to the full checklist rather than assuming.

== EDIBLE PARTS AND PREPARATION ==
- Tender tops, leaves, stems, and flowers, raw or briefly cooked. The flavor is mild, like corn silk or delicate lettuce.
- Pinch the top few inches of lush patches; the lower stems get stringy.

== SEASON ==
- A cool-season plant: lush in early spring and again in fall, and through mild winters.
- It bolts, toughens, and fades in summer heat; do not expect it in the hot dry middle of summer.

== CAUTION ==
- Contains saponins; unusually large quantities can upset the stomach. Normal salad-size portions are fine.
- Re-verify the three keys together every time: one-sided hair line, deeply split white petals, and no milky sap. The hair line alone is not enough, and milky sap is an absolute stop.
