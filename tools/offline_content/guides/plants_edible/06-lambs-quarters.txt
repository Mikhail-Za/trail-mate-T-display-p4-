Lambs Quarters (Chenopodium album)

== WHERE ==
- Disturbed, rich soil: gardens, field edges, barnyards, construction ground, and trailside waste places across most of North America.
- Late spring to frost; young tops are best in early summer, seeds in fall.

== IDENTIFY ==
- Growth: upright annual, 1 to 5 feet, often branched.
- Leaves: roughly diamond or goosefoot shaped with irregular blunt teeth.
- The signature: new growth is coated with a white, mealy powder, thickest on the youngest leaves and leaf undersides. Water beads up and rolls off a dipped leaf.
- Stem: grooved, often streaked red or purple, especially in the leaf joints.
- Flowers: dense tiny green granular clusters at the tips, no visible petals, no strong smell. The whole plant is hairless.

== LOOKALIKES ==
- Young black nightshade: TOXIC raw, and its early leaves can resemble lambs quarters before either plant flowers. Nightshade has NO white mealy coating, and it goes on to make small white star flowers with yellow centers and round berries. If the mealy coating is absent, leave it.
- Hairy or strong-smelling look-similars: not lambs quarters. Some related goosefoots smell resinous or rank; the good plant smells like nothing much.
- Orache (a saltbush relative): similar and also eaten, but apply the full checklist rather than assuming.

== EDIBLE PARTS AND PREPARATION ==
- Leaves and tender tips: cooked like spinach is best; young leaves are fine raw in moderation.
- Seeds (fall): strip the dry clusters, rub and winnow, then RINSE well to remove the bitter saponin coating, and boil like a grain. It is a close relative of quinoa.

== SEASON ==
- Leaves and tender tips: late spring into summer, with young early-summer tops the best.
- Seeds: fall, once the clusters dry; strip, winnow, and rinse well before cooking.

== CAUTION ==
- Contains oxalates like spinach; cooking reduces them. Keep portions moderate, and be careful with a history of kidney stones.
- The plant concentrates nitrates in heavily fertilized or manured soil; avoid feedlot edges and lush chemically fed ground, cook it, and do not gorge.
- As with all greens, introduce it in small amounts first.
