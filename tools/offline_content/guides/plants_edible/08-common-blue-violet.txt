Common Blue Violet (Viola sororia and close relatives)

== WHERE ==
- Lawns, moist open woods, stream banks, and shady yards; the common blue violet is widespread in eastern and central North America, with similar blue-flowered violets across much of the continent.
- Flowers in spring; tender leaves spring into early summer.

== IDENTIFY ==
- Growth: low clumps with NO upright leafy stem; every leaf and every flower rises on its own stalk directly from the base.
- Leaves: heart-shaped with scalloped, round-toothed edges; new leaves emerge rolled inward from both sides.
- Flowers: five petals, blue-violet with a white throat and fine dark veins; the two side petals are bearded with tiny hairs; each flower nods from the curled tip of its own stalk.
- Sap and smell: no milky sap, no strong odor.

== LOOKALIKES ==
- Harvest rule first: pick violets ONLY when matching flowers are present on the clump. Heart-shaped leaves alone are shared by plants you must not eat.
- Lesser celandine: TOXIC raw (a buttercup). Glossy heart-shaped leaves in similar damp ground, but its flowers are shiny YELLOW with eight or more petals. Flower check rejects it instantly.
- Wild ginger: fuzzy paired heart-shaped leaves with a spicy smell and an odd brown flower at ground level. Avoid eating it; it contains a compound linked to kidney damage and cancer.
- Yellow-flowered violets: not deadly, but can be laxative; this entry covers blue/purple-flowered plants only.

== EDIBLE PARTS AND PREPARATION ==
- Young leaves: raw in salads or cooked; they are mucilaginous and slightly thicken soups.
- Flowers: raw as a garnish or nibble, steeped as tea. Both are rich in vitamin C.

== SEASON ==
- Flowers: spring, and the harvest rule ties you to that window, since you pick only when blue-violet flowers are present on the clump.
- Leaves: spring into early summer while tender.

== CAUTION ==
- Do not eat the roots, rhizomes, or seeds; they can cause vomiting and digestive upset.
- Stick to plants verified in bloom with blue-violet flowers, and eat leaves and flowers in moderate amounts.
- In shady garden edges, be extra deliberate: ornamentals with heart-shaped leaves are common there.
