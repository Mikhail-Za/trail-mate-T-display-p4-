Acorns (Oaks, Quercus species)

== WHERE ==
- Oaks grow across most of North America: hardwood forests, savannas, canyons, and yards.
- Acorns ripen and drop in late summer and fall; gather promptly from the ground before animals and mold claim them.

== IDENTIFY ==
- The nut is the anchor feature: a smooth, hard-shelled nut seated in (or freed from) a scaly or warty CAP. Every acorn has a cap or a round cap scar. No cap, no acorn.
- Leaves: alternate and simple, usually lobed. White oak group: rounded lobes. Red oak group: pointed lobes tipped with tiny bristles. A few oaks (such as live oaks) have unlobed leaves, so rely on the acorn itself.
- Twigs: buds clustered at the tips, an oak signature.
- Bark: gray to dark, becoming furrowed or blocky with age.

== LOOKALIKES ==
- Buckeye and horse chestnut: TOXIC. Their shiny nuts show a large pale circular "eye," come packed in a thick leathery husk (smooth or spiny), and never sit in a scaly cap. Their leaves are palmate, with leaflets spread like fingers. Cap or cap scar present = acorn; big pale eye and husk = poison, leave it.
- True chestnuts are edible but arrive in a densely spiny bur; do not work from memory on chestnuts versus horse chestnuts in the field. This entry covers acorns only.

== EDIBLE PARTS AND PREPARATION ==
- Kernels only, and ONLY after leaching out the tannins. Raw bitter acorns are not food.
- Shell the nuts; discard any that are moldy, dusty inside, or insect-riddled. Chop or coarsely grind the kernels.
- Cold leach: soak in several changes of cold water over one to several days until a nibble has no bitterness. Keeps the starch better for binding breads.
- Hot leach: simmer through repeated changes of boiling water until the water stops darkening and the bitterness is gone. Red oak acorns can take hours.
- White oak group acorns are usually milder and leach faster. Dry the leached meal and use it as porridge or flour. This is one of the few genuine wild calorie staples.

== CAUTION ==
- Unleached tannins cause nausea and constipation, and heavy repeated doses can stress the kidneys. Leach until truly not bitter; taste is the test.
- Eat a small first portion of any new batch and wait before making it a meal.
