Dandelion (Taraxacum officinale)

== WHERE ==
- Lawns, fields, roadsides, trail edges, and disturbed ground across essentially all of North America.
- Leaves spring through fall (mildest in early spring); flowers heaviest in spring; roots any time the ground is workable.

== IDENTIFY ==
- Growth: a basal rosette only. Every leaf rises from one point at ground level; there are never leaves on the flower stem.
- Leaves: hairless or nearly so, deeply toothed, with the teeth pointing back toward the center of the rosette.
- Flower stem: hollow, leafless, unbranched, one flower head per stem, and it bleeds milky white sap when broken.
- Flower: a single yellow head packed with thin ray petals; closes at night and in rain; matures into the familiar white puffball seed globe.
- Root: a single taproot, brown outside, white inside, also milky.

== LOOKALIKES ==
- No deadly lookalikes, but confirm the full checklist anyway; several similar yellow-flowered weeds exist.
- Cat's ear: hairy leaves, solid branched stems, sometimes several heads per stalk. Not dangerous.
- Hawkweeds and hawksbeards: hairy leaves, branched stems with multiple flowers.
- Sow thistle: leaves grow up a tall branched stem, not just a rosette. Also considered edible.
- The test: if your plant has a branched stem, hairy leaves, leaves on the flower stalk, or multiple heads per stem, it is not a true dandelion. All checklist features must match.

== EDIBLE PARTS AND PREPARATION ==
- Young leaves: raw in small amounts (bitter) or boiled; changing the cooking water once mellows older leaves.
- Flowers: petals raw or as fritters or tea. Pinch off the green bracts under the head; they carry most of the bitterness.
- Root: scrub, chop, and roast until brown for a coffee-like brew, or boil young roots as a vegetable.
- Crown: the pale leaf base at the top of the root is good cooked.

== SEASON ==
- Leaves: best in early spring before flowering, when least bitter; still usable spring through fall.
- Flowers: spring, at peak bloom.
- Roots: dig in fall or any time the ground is workable; fall roots are richest for roasting.

== CAUTION ==
- Avoid sprayed lawns, golf courses, and busy road edges; herbicide risk outweighs the food value.
- The milky latex can irritate sensitive skin, and people with ragweed-family allergies occasionally react to eating it.
- Dandelion is a mild diuretic; keep portions moderate and stay watered.
