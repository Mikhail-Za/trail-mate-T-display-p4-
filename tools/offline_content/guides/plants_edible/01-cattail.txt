Cattail (Typha species)

== WHERE ==
- Marshes, pond and lake margins, slow ditches, and wet ground across nearly all of North America.
- Grows in dense stands rooted in shallow standing water or saturated mud.
- Useful most of the year: shoots in spring, green flower spikes and pollen in early summer, rhizomes fall through spring.

== IDENTIFY ==
- Tall: 4 to 9 feet, growing in crowded colonies.
- Leaves: long, flat, strap-like, spongy, gray-green, sheathing the stem at the base in a rounded, cylindrical bundle (never a flat fan).
- Seed head: the unmistakable brown sausage-shaped spike on a stiff round stalk. Old heads persist through winter and burst into fluff.
- Cut shoot base: layered white core like a leek, round to crescent in cross-section, with a mild cucumber-like smell.
- Habitat itself is a feature: feet in water or muck, full sun.

== LOOKALIKES ==
- Irises (blue flag, yellow flag): TOXIC, and they grow in the same wet ground. Iris leaves rise in a FLAT FAN from the base, and iris never produces a brown sausage head; its flowers are showy purple or yellow. If the leaf bases form a flattened fan, walk away.
- Sweet flag: not deadly, but different. Crushed leaves smell strongly sweet and spicy, and its flower spike juts off the side of a leaf-like stalk. Cattail smells mild.
- Safety rule: harvest young shoots only from stands that show old or current brown seed heads, so you know what colony you are standing in.

== EDIBLE PARTS AND PREPARATION ==
- Young shoots (spring): grasp the inner leaves of a young plant and pull; peel to the tender white core. Eat raw only from clean water, otherwise cook briefly.
- Green flower spikes (early summer, before pollen sheds): boil and eat like corn on the cob.
- Pollen (early summer): bend the yellow-dusted spike into a bag and shake; mix the pollen into flour or porridge.
- Rhizomes (best fall to spring): peel, then crush in clean water and let the starch settle, or roast and chew the starch off the fibers. One of the few real calorie sources in this guide.

== CAUTION ==
- Cattails absorb pollutants from the water they grow in. Do not harvest from roadside ditches, farm runoff, or any suspect water.
- When in doubt about water quality, cook everything and skip the raw shoots.
- Repeat the iris rule every single time: flat fan of leaves means no.
