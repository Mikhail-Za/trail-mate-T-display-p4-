Blueberry and Huckleberry (Vaccinium and Gaylussacia)

== WHERE ==
- Acidic soils: pine barrens, rocky mountain slopes, bog edges, burned-over land, and open woods. Lowbush and highbush blueberries in the East and North; huckleberries especially in western mountains and eastern woods.
- Flowers in spring; fruit ripens mid to late summer.

== IDENTIFY ==
- Demand ALL of these together; each one rules out a dangerous plant.
- Growth: a woody SHRUB, knee-high to head-high. Never a vine, never a soft-stemmed herb.
- Leaves: simple, alternate, short-stalked, smooth-edged or very finely toothed.
- Flowers: small white-to-pink URN or bell shapes with a narrowed mouth, hanging in clusters. Where flowers are gone, look for them on late bushes nearby.
- Berry: blue to blue-black (some huckleberries shiny black), often wearing a whitish waxy bloom, with a five-pointed CROWN (a star-shaped scar) on the end opposite the stem.
- Inside: pale greenish-white flesh that does not stain purple, with many tiny soft seeds (blueberry) or about ten small crunchy seeds (huckleberry). Familiar sweet taste.

== LOOKALIKES ==
- Virginia creeper: TOXIC blue berries, but on a VINE with five-fingered leaves and bright red-pink fruit stalks. No crown. The shrub rule rejects it.
- Pokeweed: TOXIC purple-black berries in long grape-like clusters on magenta stems of a tall fleshy herb; juice stains everything purple. No crown, wrong plant form.
- Common buckthorn: black berries on a shrub or small tree, but with no crown and two to four hard seeds; strongly laxative and toxic.
- Nightshades: shiny berries on weak herbs or scrambling vines; no crown, no urn flowers.
- If even one required feature is missing, especially the crown, it is not your berry.

== EDIBLE PARTS AND PREPARATION ==
- Fully ripe berries raw, cooked, or dried. Nothing else on the plant is used here.

== SEASON ==
- Flowers: spring, the small urn-shaped bells.
- Fruit: ripens mid to late summer; take only deeply colored, fully ripe berries still wearing their waxy bloom.

== CAUTION ==
- Eat only deeply colored, fully ripe fruit.
- First harvest in a new region: eat a few, wait several hours, then continue.
- Leave vine-borne and cluster-borne berries alone entirely; the shrub-plus-crown combination is the whole safety case.
