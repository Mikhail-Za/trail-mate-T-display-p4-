Ticks, Insects, and Spiders

== TICKS ==
- Habitat: tall grass, brush, leaf litter, and the edge zone between woods and field. Ticks quest by waiting on vegetation and grabbing what brushes past; they do not jump or drop from trees.
- Prevention: treat clothing, boots, and gear with 0.5 percent permethrin (clothing ONLY, never skin; let it dry first). Use DEET 20 to 30 percent or picaridin 20 percent on exposed skin. Wear light colors, tuck pants into socks, stay in the center of trails.
- Do a full-body check every day in tick season: hairline, in and behind ears, armpits, waistband, groin, behind knees, between toes. Check kids and dogs too.
- Correct removal: fine-tipped tweezers, grip as close to the skin as possible, pull straight up with slow steady pressure. No twisting, burning, petroleum jelly, or nail polish; those make the tick regurgitate into the wound.
- Clean the site with soap and water or alcohol. Note the date. Save the tick in a bag or take a photo for identification.
- Lyme disease: watch 3 to 30 days for an expanding red rash, often a bullseye with central clearing (erythema migrans). Important: the rash is absent in a meaningful fraction of cases, so also watch for fever, chills, headache, fatigue, and joint or muscle aches.
- Seek care promptly for any rash or fever after a tick bite, especially if the tick was attached more than 24 to 36 hours. Sudden high fever with headache or spotted rash after a bite can be Rocky Mountain spotted fever; that is urgent.

== MOSQUITOES ==
- Disease note: in North America mosquitoes can carry West Nile virus and, less commonly, other encephalitis viruses. Most bites are only itchy, but repellent is cheap insurance.
- Peak activity is dawn and dusk; plan camp chores accordingly. Camp away from standing water and marsh edges.
- DEET or picaridin on skin, permethrin on clothes, head net when they are thick.

== BLACK WIDOW ==
- Glossy black spider with a red hourglass on the UNDERSIDE of the abdomen; messy, strong web low to the ground.
- Habitat: woodpiles, outhouses, under rocks and logs, crawl spaces, stored gear.
- Bite: pinprick, then within an hour severe muscle cramping, rigid abdomen, sweating. Rarely fatal but painful; get medical care.

== BROWN RECLUSE ==
- Range: mainly the south-central and midwestern US. Uniform tan-brown, about quarter-sized with legs, a dark violin shape on the back (unreliable alone) and six eyes in three pairs.
- Habitat: dry undisturbed spaces: stored boxes, boots, gloves, bedding, woodpiles. Shake out boots, clothes, and sleeping bags in its range.
- Bite is often painless at first; some develop a slow necrotic ulcer over days. Persistent worsening wounds need medical care.

== FIRE ANTS ==
- Southeastern US and Texas: soft dome mounds with no visible entrance. Disturb one and workers swarm silently up your leg, then all sting at once.
- Brush them off fast with your hand and move well away; water does not remove them. Stings form itchy pustules; do not pop them.
- Multiple stings or any wheezing, swelling of the face, or dizziness means possible anaphylaxis: use an epinephrine auto-injector if available and evacuate.

== AFRICANIZED BEES ==
- Southern US. Same venom as ordinary honey bees but they defend the hive in far greater numbers and pursue a quarter mile or more.
- If attacked: RUN, far and fast, in a straight line toward shelter (building, vehicle). Cover your face and eyes with your shirt as you go.
- Do NOT jump into water; the swarm waits above the surface and stings you each time you breathe.
- Do not swat or flail; crushed bees release alarm scent that draws more. Once safe, scrape stingers out sideways with a fingernail or card as fast as possible; speed matters more than method.

== SEASON ==
- Ticks are most active spring through early fall: a strong peak from spring into early summer (tiny nymphs) and a second rise in fall (adults). In mild regions adults quest on any thawed winter day, so check year-round.
- Mosquitoes run the warm months, worst at dawn and dusk and after rains fill standing water.
- Fire ants and Africanized bees are most aggressive in warm weather. Black widow and brown recluse spiders shelter in gear and buildings year-round, so shake out boots and bags in any season.
