Bear Encounters and Safety

== SPECIES ID: BLACK VS GRIZZLY ==
- Do NOT use color. Black bears can be brown, cinnamon, or blond. Grizzlies can be dark.
- Grizzly: prominent shoulder hump (muscle mass above front legs), dished or concave face profile, short rounded ears, front claws 2 to 4 inches long.
- Black bear: no shoulder hump (rump is the highest point), straight "Roman" face profile from forehead to nose, taller and more pointed ears, claws under 2 inches.
- Size overlaps; a big black bear can outweigh a small grizzly. Use hump, face, ears, and claws together, never one feature alone.
- Range check: grizzlies live mainly in Alaska, western Canada, and the northern Rockies (MT, WY, ID, small parts of WA). East of the Rockies and in most of the lower 48, assume black bear.

== PREVENTION ==
- Make noise on the trail: talk, clap, call out near streams, blind corners, and dense brush. Most attacks come from surprising a bear at close range.
- Travel in groups when possible. Groups of 3 or more are rarely attacked.
- Never leave food, trash, or scented items (toothpaste, sunscreen, wrappers) in your tent or pack overnight.
- Store food in a bear canister, approved bear bag hang (12 feet up, 6 feet from the trunk), or a provided locker.
- Camp triangle: cooking area, food storage, and sleeping area each 100 yards apart, with the tent upwind of the cooking site.
- Cook and eat away from your tent. Sleep in different clothes than you cooked in.

== ENCOUNTER: BLACK BEAR ==
- If the bear has not noticed you, back away quietly the way you came.
- If it sees you: stand tall, wave your arms, speak firmly, look big. Do not run and do not climb a tree; black bears do both better than you.
- Back away slowly at an angle. Give it an exit route.
- If a black bear attacks and makes contact: FIGHT BACK. Hit the face and nose with anything available. Do not play dead with a black bear.

== ENCOUNTER: GRIZZLY ==
- Stand your ground, speak calmly, avoid direct eye contact, back away slowly. Never run.
- Bluff charges are common: ears up, bounding, veering off. Hold your ground and keep talking.
- Defensive attack (you surprised it, or it has cubs or a carcass): PLAY DEAD. Lie face down, hands laced over your neck, elbows and legs spread wide so it cannot flip you. Stay still until the bear is long gone.
- Predatory attack (rare: bear stalks you, keeps coming, or attacks at night in your tent): FIGHT BACK with everything you have. Playing dead is wrong for a predatory bear.

== BEAR SPRAY ==
- More effective than firearms in studies. Carry it on your hip or chest strap, not in your pack.
- Effective range is roughly 15 to 30 feet. Know your canister's rating.
- Remove the safety clip, aim slightly downward, and sweep side to side to put a cloud between you and the charging bear.
- Spray in 1 to 2 second bursts starting when the bear is about 30 to 60 feet out. Mind the wind; a headwind blows it back on you.
- After spraying, leave the area immediately. Do not linger.

== CUBS ==
- A cub anywhere near you means the mother is close and this is the most dangerous bear situation there is.
- Do not photograph, approach, or try to locate the mother. Back away the direction you came and leave the area NOW.
