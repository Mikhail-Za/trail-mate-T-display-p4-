WATER HEMLOCK: DO NOT EAT

Water hemlock (Cicuta species) is often called the most
poisonous plant in North America. A piece of root the size of
a walnut can kill an adult. It grows near water across the
continent. The root is the deadliest part. Never taste, chew,
or eat any part.

This page is built to be used from the top down. If someone
has just eaten it, work the first sections NOW. The ID and
avoidance sections lower down are how you keep it from
happening in the first place, and for this plant avoidance is
the only reliable protection.

== IF SOMEONE HAS EATEN IT: DO THIS NOW ==
- Get all plant material out of the mouth. Have them spit.
  Wipe or rinse the mouth with water. Every bit not swallowed
  is poison that cannot hurt them.
- Do NOT make them vomit. No finger down the throat, no salt
  water, no ipecac. Vomiting comes on its own, and forcing it
  risks choking, especially once seizures start.
- Move them NOW to a flat, safe spot away from water, fire,
  drop-offs, and hard or sharp objects. Seizures often hit in
  15 to 60 minutes, sometimes sooner. A person seizing beside
  a stream or ditch drowns fast.
- Note what was eaten, how much, and when. Stay with them.
  Do not leave them alone.

== ACTIVATED CHARCOAL: ONE REAL OPTION IF YOU CARRY IT ==
- Only real pharmacy activated charcoal counts. Campfire char,
  ash, and burnt toast do NOT work. Do not rely on them.
- Give it ONLY if the person is fully awake, able to swallow,
  and not yet vomiting or twitching. Earliest is best, ideally
  within the first hour.
- Adult dose is roughly 50 grams (about 1 gram per kg)
  stirred into water.
  Use the package amount for a child.
- Charcoal can soak up poison still in the gut and lower the
  dose the body absorbs. It is not a cure.
- Do NOT give it to anyone drowsy, confused, vomiting, or
  seizing. It will go into the lungs and can kill.

== IF SEIZURES START: THIS IS WHERE LIVES ARE SAVED ==
- Do not restrain them. Holding a seizing person down causes
  injuries and does not stop the seizure.
- Do not put anything in the mouth. They will not swallow the
  tongue. Objects break teeth and block the airway.
- Clear hard and sharp things away. Cushion the head with a
  pack or rolled clothing. Loosen anything tight at the neck.
- Keep them clear of water. Drowning during a seizure is a
  real and fast killer with this plant.
- The instant a seizure eases, roll them onto their side
  (recovery position). This drains saliva and vomit out of
  the mouth instead of into the lungs. This single action
  saves more lives here than anything else you can do.
- Keep them on their side between seizures and until fully
  awake. Watch the breathing without pause.
- Seizures may come in waves. Ride each one out the same way.
- If breathing stops and stays stopped after a seizure ends,
  and you know rescue breathing or CPR, start it at once. It
  is the only tool you have if breathing quits. Be honest:
  against a large dose it may not be enough, but it is a real
  chance and worth giving.
- Shield them from cold ground, sun, and rain the whole time.
  A body worn down by seizures loses heat fast.

== AFTER THE SEIZURES ==
- Keep them on their side and keep watching. More seizures
  can return for hours.
- Once fully alert and able to swallow, let them sip water.
  Vomiting and sweating cost a lot of fluid.
- Dark, cola-colored urine means muscle breakdown from the
  seizures. Have them drink water steadily if they can.
- Rest. Do not let them walk hard terrain until clearly
  steady. Recovery can take days.

== THE HARD TRUTH ==
- There is no antidote for water hemlock. Nothing you can
  forage, brew, or improvise neutralizes the toxin
  (cicutoxin). Anyone claiming a bush cure is gambling with
  a life.
- A full mouthful of root drives seizures and breathing
  failure that can kill even in a hospital with sedative
  drugs and a ventilator. In the field with none of that, a
  large dose may not be survivable, and no remedy changes
  that. Do not put other people at risk chasing a cure that
  does not exist.
- Here is the real hope: the body breaks the poison down over
  hours. People who get through the seizure phase with the
  airway kept open usually recover well, with no lasting
  seizure disorder. So the whole job is: lower the dose early
  if you safely can, then keep the airway clear and the body
  unhurt until the toxin burns off. That, not any medicine
  you lack, is what actually saves the life.

== IF YOU CAN REACH HELP ==
- The definitive treatment is sedative seizure drugs
  (benzodiazepines such as diazepam, lorazepam, or midazolam)
  and breathing support. Those live in an ambulance or
  hospital, not in a pack.
- Evacuate if you can safely reach help. This is one option,
  not the first thing to do.
- Do the airway and safety basics first and without pause.
  Never delay rolling someone onto their side to run for a
  signal.
- If you have a second person, send them for help so someone
  always stays to guard the airway. Do not leave a seizing
  person alone.

== HABITAT ==
- Wet ground: marshes, ditches, stream and pond edges, wet
  meadows.
- Found in most of North America, often in the open near
  water.
- Because it grows where people gather water or greens,
  contact is more likely than its rarity suggests.

== IDENTIFICATION ==
- Grows 2 to 6 feet tall on a smooth, hollow, branching stem.
- Stem is pale green, often streaked or mottled with purple.
- Flowers form compound umbels: many small white flowers in
  umbrella-like clusters made of smaller clusters.
- Leaves are divided into toothed leaflets. The side veins
  often run to the NOTCHES between the teeth, not to the
  tips. "Vein to the cut, pain to the gut" is the memory aid.
- Rootstock is thick and, when cut lengthwise, shows hollow
  chambers separated by partitions. Cut roots release a
  yellowish, oily liquid that smells like raw parsnip or
  carrot. The chambered root is the single most reliable
  feature. Do NOT cut or handle the root to test this;
  observe only, and wash hands if you touch it.
- Root and tubers are most toxic, worst in early spring.
  Leaves and stems are also toxic in early growth.

== HOW IT DIFFERS FROM POISON HEMLOCK ==
- Both are deadly. Both have white umbel flowers.
- Water hemlock: branched, tuberous roots with the chambered,
  partitioned rootstock. Purple is streaked or mottled on the
  stem.
- Poison hemlock: a single carrot-like taproot, no chambers,
  and purple blotches or spots on a stem with a musty, mousy
  smell.
- You do not need to tell them apart to stay safe. Avoid both.

== WHY IT KILLS ==
- The toxin cicutoxin blocks the brain's braking signal
  (GABA), so nerves fire without a stop, driving violent
  seizures.
- Symptoms can begin within 15 to 60 minutes, sometimes
  sooner: nausea, vomiting, heavy drooling, sweating, dilated
  pupils, then repeated grand-mal seizures.
- Death follows from nonstop seizures and breathing failure,
  and can come within hours.
- There is no simple antidote. Field care is protecting the
  airway and body through the seizures (see the sections
  above).

== THE MIMICRY PROBLEM ==
- Water hemlock is in the carrot and parsley family
  (Apiaceae).
- That family includes wild carrot (Queen Anne's lace), wild
  parsnip, water parsley, and cow parsnip, plus deadly poison
  hemlock. Many look confusingly alike with white umbel
  flowers and divided leaves.
- Edible members exist, but the deadly ones are easy to
  confuse, and a single mistake can be fatal.

== THE RULE ==
- Avoid ALL wild plants with lacy, umbrella-shaped clusters
  of tiny white flowers and finely divided leaves.
- Do not forage anything in this family in the field. The
  safe members are not worth the risk of a fatal
  misidentification.
- Keep children and pets away from wet-ground umbel plants.
  If one may have chewed a root or stem, work the emergency
  sections at the top of this page at once.
