WATER HEMLOCK: DO NOT EAT

Water hemlock (Cicuta species) is often called the most poisonous
plant in North America. A small amount can kill an adult. It grows
near water across the continent. Never taste, chew, or eat any part.

== HABITAT ==
- Wet ground: marshes, ditches, stream and pond edges, wet meadows.
- Found in most of North America, often in the open near water.
- Because it grows where people gather water or greens, contact is
  more likely than its rarity suggests.

== IDENTIFICATION ==
- Grows 2 to 6 feet tall on a smooth, hollow, branching stem.
- Stem is often streaked or mottled with purple.
- Flowers form compound umbels: many small white flowers in
  umbrella-like clusters made of smaller clusters.
- Leaves are divided into toothed leaflets; leaf veins often end
  at the notches between teeth rather than at the tips.
- Rootstock is thick and, when cut lengthwise, shows hollow
  chambers separated by partitions. Cut roots release a yellowish,
  oily liquid that smells like raw parsnip or carrot. Do NOT cut
  or handle the root to test this; observe only.

== WHY IT KILLS ==
- The toxin cicutoxin attacks the nervous system fast.
- Symptoms can begin within 15 to 60 minutes: nausea, vomiting,
  drooling, then violent, repeated seizures.
- Death can follow quickly from seizures and breathing failure.
- There is no simple antidote; care is emergency hospital support.
- Treat any suspected ingestion as a life-threatening emergency
  and evacuate immediately (see the poisoning-response file).

== THE MIMICRY PROBLEM ==
- Water hemlock is in the carrot and parsley family (Apiaceae).
- That family includes wild carrot (Queen Anne's lace), wild
  parsnip, water parsley, and cow parsnip, plus deadly poison
  hemlock. Many look confusingly alike with white umbel flowers
  and divided leaves.
- Edible members exist, but the deadly ones are easy to confuse,
  and a single mistake can be fatal.

== THE RULE ==
- Avoid ALL wild plants with lacy, umbrella-shaped clusters of
  tiny white flowers and finely divided leaves.
- Do not forage anything in this family in the field. The safe
  members are not worth the risk of a fatal misidentification.
- If children or pets may have chewed a wet-ground umbel plant,
  treat it as an emergency and seek help at once.
