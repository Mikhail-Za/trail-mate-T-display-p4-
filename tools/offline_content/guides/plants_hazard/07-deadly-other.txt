OTHER DEADLY PLANTS

These plants poison if eaten and several are found around homes,
gardens, and disturbed ground. All parts should be treated as
unsafe. Keep children and pets away.

== JIMSONWEED / DATURA ==
- Coarse plant with large white to purple trumpet flowers and
  spiny seed pods holding many dark seeds; foul-smelling leaves.
- Grows in disturbed soil, fields, and roadsides across North
  America.
- All parts, especially seeds, cause anticholinergic poisoning:
  dry mouth, flushed skin, blurred vision, dilated pupils, racing
  heart, high fever, agitation, hallucinations, delirium, and
  possible death. Do not eat, chew, or brew any part.

== WHITE SNAKEROOT ==
- Woodland plant, 1 to 4 feet, with clusters of small fluffy white
  flowers and toothed, pointed opposite leaves.
- Contains tremetol. Cattle that graze it pass the toxin into milk,
  which historically caused "milk sickness" and many human deaths,
  including reportedly the mother of Abraham Lincoln.
- Toxic to people and livestock. Do not eat.

== CASTOR BEAN ==
- Large ornamental with star-shaped leaves and spiny seed pods
  holding mottled, bean-like seeds.
- Seeds contain ricin, one of the most potent plant toxins; a few
  chewed seeds can kill. Escapes gardens into warm regions.
- Never handle or eat the seeds; keep them away from children.

== FOXGLOVE ==
- Tall spike of tubular, thimble-shaped flowers, often pink,
  purple, or white with spotted throats; soft fuzzy leaves.
- Contains heart-affecting toxins (cardiac glycosides). Causes
  nausea, vision changes, and dangerous heart rhythm problems.
- All parts are toxic. Do not eat; do not mistake leaves for
  edible greens.

== OLEANDER ==
- Evergreen shrub with narrow leathery leaves and clusters of pink,
  white, or red flowers; common in warmer southern ranges.
- Extremely toxic; all parts affect the heart. Smoke from burning
  it and even water it has stood in can be harmful.
- Never eat any part or use the stems as skewers.

== HORSE CHESTNUT vs EDIBLE CHESTNUT ==
- Horse chestnut (buckeye) is TOXIC; true sweet chestnut is edible.
- Husk: horse chestnut has a thick husk with few, thick, widely
  spaced spines; true chestnut has a dense husk covered in many
  fine, sharp spines like a hedgehog.
- Nuts: horse chestnut usually holds one smooth, rounded nut; true
  chestnut holds two to three flatter nuts with a tassel-like tip.
- Leaves: horse chestnut has leaflets spread like fingers from one
  point; true chestnut has single long, toothed leaves.
- If unsure, do not eat it.
