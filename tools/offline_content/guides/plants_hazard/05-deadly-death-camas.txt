DEATH CAMAS vs WILD ONION

Death camas (Toxicoscordion, formerly Zigadenus) is a classic
killer of foragers because it grows among, and looks like, wild
onions and edible blue camas. All parts are poisonous, and the
toxin is not destroyed by cooking or drying. As few as two bulbs
can kill an adult. The single best protection is prevention: do
not eat any onion-like or camas-like bulb unless you are certain.
This file tells you how to be certain, and what to do if someone
has already eaten it.

== IF SOMEONE MAY HAVE EATEN IT: DO THIS NOW ==
Act in this order. There is no antidote, so field care is about
protecting the airway and buying time (see THE HARD TRUTH).
- Stop the exposure. Spit out any plant. Rinse the mouth with
  water and spit, then wipe out any residue.
- Do NOT force vomiting. It rarely removes enough to help, and it
  risks choking, because this poison brings on weakness,
  drowsiness, and sometimes seizures. If the body vomits on its
  own, let it.
- Save a piece of the plant if you can. It helps anyone who later
  treats the patient.

== SECOND OPTION: BLOCK ABSORPTION ==
- If you carry activated charcoal AND the person is fully awake,
  able to swallow, and not vomiting: give it. It can bind toxin
  still in the gut. Best within about 1 hour of eating.
- Adult dose: about 25 to 50 grams stirred into water.
- Child dose: about 1 gram per kg of body weight.
- Do NOT give charcoal to anyone who is sleepy, confused, seizing,
  or vomiting hard. They can inhale it and drown. When unsure,
  skip it and protect the airway instead.

== THIRD OPTION: SUPPORT THE BODY ==
Protect the airway (the top survival step):
- Vomiting plus weakness can choke a person. Roll them onto their
  side (recovery position), head low and turned so vomit drains
  out, not in. Never leave them face-up or alone.
Hold up the heart and blood pressure:
- This poison slows the heart and drops blood pressure. Lay the
  person flat and raise the legs about a foot to feed the brain.
- Keep them still and warm, and insulate them from the ground.
  Any exertion strains a struggling heart. Do NOT let a
  symptomatic person walk.
Give fluids (a partial stand-in for a hospital IV drip):
- If they are fully alert and not vomiting, give small frequent
  sips of water, or water with a pinch of salt and sugar, to
  replace losses and support blood pressure. Stop if they get
  drowsy or start vomiting.
If breathing or the heart stops:
- Give rescue breaths if they are not breathing. If there is no
  pulse and you are trained, start CPR and continue as long as you
  are able. In severe slowing of the heart this is the only field
  tool left.
Watch closely:
- Recheck pulse and alertness every few minutes. Danger signs are
  a pulse that keeps slowing, fainting, confusion, or seizures.
- Symptoms usually begin within 30 minutes to a few hours. In
  people who survive, they ease within about 24 hours. Your job is
  to get them through the first day.

== THE HARD TRUTH: NO FIELD ANTIDOTE ==
- Nothing in a pack reverses this poison. The tools that do
  (atropine to speed the heart, IV fluids and drugs to hold up
  blood pressure) exist only at a hospital.
- A large dose can cause a fatal slow or irregular heartbeat that
  field care cannot fix. There is no folk remedy that works.
  Chasing one wastes the time you need for real care.
- What field care does buy you: it stops choking, slows the
  crisis, and keeps the brain and heart supplied while the body
  clears the toxin. Most people who survive the first 24 hours
  recover fully.
- Evacuate if you can safely reach help. This is one of the few
  poisonings where a hospital truly changes the odds, so if a
  realistic path to care exists, move toward it, carried rather
  than self-walking if symptoms have started. If no evacuation is
  possible, the steps above are your full and best effort. Do them
  well, and do not give up.

== PREVENTION: THE NO-ONION-SMELL RULE ==
- True wild onions and wild garlic (Allium) smell strongly of
  onion or garlic when a leaf or bulb is crushed.
- Death camas has NO onion or garlic smell.
- Rule: if it looks like a wild onion but does NOT smell like one,
  do not eat it. No smell, no eat.
- Limit of the rule: it separates death camas from onions only.
  Edible blue camas also has no onion smell, so smell will NOT
  tell death camas from blue camas. For that you need flowers
  (see below).
- Trap: a crushed onion nearby can leave scent on your hands and
  fool you. Smell a fresh, clean sample. Any doubt at all: do not
  eat it.

== FLOWER DIFFERENCES ==
- Wild onion: flowers in a rounded cluster at the top of one
  stalk, often pink, white, or lavender, sometimes with tiny
  bulblets mixed in, and an oniony smell.
- Blue camas (edible): star-shaped flowers, blue to purple.
- Death camas: flowers cream, white, or greenish-yellow in a long
  narrow cluster (a spike or raceme), with six petal-like
  segments, often with a small gland spot near the base of each.
- All three have narrow, grass-like leaves, which is exactly why
  flowers and smell matter so much.
- Bulbs look alike underground. You cannot tell them apart by the
  bulb, so never dig and eat when the plant is not in flower.

== HABITAT OVERLAP ==
- Death camas grows in meadows, grasslands, open woods, foothills,
  and prairies across much of North America, especially the West.
- Wild onions and blue camas share these same open, grassy places,
  so the plants are commonly found side by side.
- They bloom at similar times, and before flowering they are very
  hard to tell apart.

== WHY IT IS DEADLY ==
- The toxins (zygacine and related steroidal alkaloids) jam the
  sodium channels in nerve and muscle and drive up vagal (heart-
  slowing) signals to the heart and gut.
- Early: numb or tingling lips and mouth, watering mouth, nausea.
- Then: vomiting, belly pain, weakness, dizziness, staggering,
  slowed heartbeat, and low blood pressure.
- Severe: confusion, seizures, a very slow or irregular heart,
  coma, and death. Livestock and people have died from small
  amounts.

== THE SAFE RULE ==
- Do not eat any onion-like or camas-like plant unless it clearly
  smells of onion or garlic AND you are certain of its ID.
- No smell, or any doubt at all: leave it. No wild bulb is worth a
  fatal mistake.
