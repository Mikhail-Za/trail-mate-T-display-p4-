DEATH CAMAS vs WILD ONION

Death camas (Toxicoscordion and related genera) is a classic killer
of foragers because it grows among and looks like wild onions and
edible camas bulbs. All parts are poisonous. Eating the bulb can be
fatal. This is one of the most important pairs to get right, so the
safe move is to skip wild onions entirely unless expertly trained.

== THE NO-ONION-SMELL RULE ==
- True wild onions and wild garlic smell strongly of onion or
  garlic when a leaf or bulb is crushed.
- Death camas has NO onion or garlic smell.
- Rule: if it looks like a wild onion but does NOT smell like one,
  do not eat it. No smell means no eat.
- Caution: a nearby crushed onion can leave the scent on your
  hands and fool you. Smell a fresh, clean sample, and when in any
  doubt, do not eat it.

== FLOWER DIFFERENCES ==
- Wild onion: flowers form a rounded cluster at the top of a single
  stalk, often pink, white, or lavender, sometimes with small
  bulblets mixed in.
- Death camas: flowers are cream, white, or greenish-yellow in an
  elongated cluster (a spike or narrow raceme) along the upper
  stem, with six petal-like segments, often with a small gland
  spot near the base of each segment.
- Leaves of both are grass-like and narrow, which is exactly why
  flowers and smell matter so much.
- Bulbs look alike underground; you cannot tell them apart by the
  bulb, so never rely on the bulb for ID.

== HABITAT OVERLAP ==
- Death camas grows in meadows, grasslands, open woods, foothills,
  and prairies across much of North America, especially the West.
- Wild onions grow in many of the same open, grassy places, so the
  two are commonly found side by side.
- They may bloom at similar times, and before flowering the two are
  very hard to separate.

== WHY IT KILLS ==
- The toxins (zygacine and related alkaloids) cause vomiting,
  drooling, weakness, slowed heartbeat, low blood pressure,
  confusion, and can progress to death.
- Livestock and people have died from small amounts.
- Any suspected ingestion is a medical emergency; evacuate and get
  help at once (see the poisoning-response file).

== THE SAFE RULE ==
- Do not eat any wild onion-like plant unless it clearly smells of
  onion or garlic AND you are certain of its ID.
- If there is no smell, or any doubt at all, leave it. The risk of
  a fatal mistake outweighs a wild onion.
