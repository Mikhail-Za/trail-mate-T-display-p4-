OTHER CONTACT HAZARDS

Some plants injure through stings, burning sap, or embedded spines.
These reactions can be painful and, with a few plants, severe.

== STINGING NETTLE ==
- ID: upright plant 2 to 6 feet tall; opposite, toothed, heart-
  shaped to lance-shaped leaves; square stem; tiny hairs on stems
  and leaf undersides; small greenish flower clusters.
- Habitat: moist soil, stream banks, disturbed ground, shade.
- The hairs inject a stinging fluid causing burning, redness, and
  raised bumps that usually fade within hours to a day.
- Treatment: do not rub. Rinse with water; remove hairs with tape.
  A paste of baking soda or a cool compress helps. Antihistamines
  ease itching. Reactions are usually mild but can bother sensitive
  people longer.
- Note: stinging nettle is edible when cooked; heat destroys the
  sting. See the foraging notes elsewhere in this guide. Never eat
  it raw, and be fully certain of ID before eating any wild plant.

== WILD PARSNIP AND GIANT HOGWEED ==
- These cause phytophotodermatitis: sap on skin plus sunlight
  triggers chemical burns and blisters, often hours later.
- Wild parsnip: 2 to 5 feet tall; flat-topped clusters of small
  YELLOW flowers; grooved stem; leaves with toothed leaflets.
- Giant hogweed: very large, 8 to 14 feet tall; huge umbrella-
  shaped clusters of white flowers; thick green stem with purple
  blotches and coarse hairs; leaves up to 5 feet wide. Rare but
  serious; the burns can be deep and scarring.
- If sap touches skin: get OUT of sunlight immediately. Wash the
  area with soap and water at once. Keep the skin covered and out
  of sun for at least 48 hours.
- If sap reaches the eyes, rinse with water and shield from light.
  Seek medical care for eye contact or large blisters.

== CACTUS SPINE REMOVAL ==
- Large spines: pull straight out with tweezers or pliers.
- Fine hair-like spines (glochids) from prickly pear are worse;
  they lodge in skin and break off.
- Remove glochids by pressing tape or a thin layer of white glue
  over the area, letting it set, then peeling it off with the
  spines. A fine comb can lift larger clusters.
- Clean the area, watch for infection, and do not squeeze the skin.

== SEASON ==
- These are warm-season hazards, worst spring through fall when
  the plants are green and growing.
- Wild parsnip and giant hogweed burns peak in summer: strong
  sunlight plus fresh sap drives the reaction.
- Stinging nettle stings hardest on lush spring and summer
  growth. Prickly pear glochids are a year-round risk.

== GENERAL ==
- When unsure, do not touch. Long sleeves and gloves prevent most
  contact injuries on the trail.
