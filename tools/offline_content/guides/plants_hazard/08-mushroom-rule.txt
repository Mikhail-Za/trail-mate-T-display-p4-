THE NO WILD MUSHROOM RULE

Do not eat any wild mushroom in the field. Not one. This guide will
not help you decide a mushroom is safe, and no short text can. This
rule is stated hard on purpose, because the mistakes are deadly.

== WHY TEXT ID CANNOT MAKE A MUSHROOM SAFE ==
- The deadliest mushrooms look plain and ordinary. The destroying
  angel is a smooth white mushroom; the death cap is a plain
  greenish to brownish cap. Both resemble edible species and even
  ordinary lawn or store mushrooms to an untrained eye.
- Safe identification needs multiple features seen in person: cap,
  gills, stem, a ring, a cup (volva) at the base often hidden
  underground, spore print color, smell, habitat, and the host
  tree. A photo or word list cannot capture all of this reliably.
- Regional variation and look-alikes defeat simple rules. There is
  NO valid shortcut such as "peels easily," "grows on wood,"
  "animals eat it," "smells nice," or "silver spoon turns black."
  Every one of these folk tests is false and has killed people.

== AMATOXINS: THE HIDDEN KILLERS ==
- The death cap and destroying angel contain amatoxins.
- Symptoms are DELAYED, typically 6 to 24 hours after eating, long
  after the toxin has been absorbed.
- There is often a false recovery: symptoms ease for a day, then
  return worse as the liver and kidneys fail.
- A single cap can kill an adult. Cooking, drying, and freezing do
  NOT destroy amatoxins.

== THE MATH ==
- A wild mushroom offers a few calories at most.
- The downside of one wrong pick is liver failure, transplant, or
  death. No meal is worth that trade. Carry real food instead.

== IF SOMEONE ATE A WILD MUSHROOM ==
- Treat it as an emergency even if the person feels fine. Feeling
  fine early means nothing with amatoxins.
- SAVE A SAMPLE: keep any uneaten mushroom and any pieces, dry in a
  paper bag (not plastic), and photograph it where it grew,
  including the base. This helps doctors identify the toxin.
- Do NOT induce vomiting unless told to by a medical professional.
- Note the time it was eaten and when symptoms start.
- Evacuate toward help NOW. Call poison control at 1-800-222-1222
  once back in phone coverage, and get to a hospital.
- Warn others: if the mushrooms were shared, everyone who ate them
  needs evaluation, even those without symptoms yet.

== THE RULE, RESTATED ==
- When it comes to wild mushrooms on the trail: do not eat them.
