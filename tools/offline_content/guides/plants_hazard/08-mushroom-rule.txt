THE NO WILD MUSHROOM RULE

Do not eat any wild mushroom in the field. Not one. This guide will
not help you decide a mushroom is safe, and no short text can. This
rule is stated hard on purpose, because the mistakes are deadly and
the deadliest ones have no cure you can carry.

== YOUR STRONGEST MOVE IS PREVENTION ==
- The one action that saves you is simple: do not eat wild fungi.
  This is a decision you control completely, with nothing but
  self-discipline. It beats every treatment below.
- A healthy person survives many days, often weeks, with no food.
  You will not starve before rescue or self-rescue. Hunger is not an
  emergency. A poisoned liver is.
- Ration and stretch the real food you carry. Prioritize water,
  warmth, and calories you KNOW are safe (packed food, fish, clearly
  known plants). A wild mushroom offers a few calories and risks
  your life. That is a losing trade every time.

== WHY TEXT ID CANNOT MAKE A MUSHROOM SAFE ==
- The deadliest mushrooms look plain and ordinary. The destroying
  angel is a smooth white mushroom. The death cap is a plain
  greenish to brownish cap. Both resemble edible species and even
  store mushrooms to an untrained eye.
- Safe ID needs many features seen in person: cap, gills, stem, a
  ring, a cup (volva) at the base often buried, spore print color,
  smell, habitat, and host tree. A photo or word list cannot capture
  all of this reliably.
- Every folk shortcut is FALSE and has killed people: "peels
  easily," "grows on wood," "animals eat it," "smells nice,"
  "silver spoon turns black," "cook it long enough." None work.
  Cooking, drying, and freezing do NOT destroy the deadly toxins.

== AMATOXINS: THE HIDDEN KILLERS ==
- Death cap, destroying angel, and some Galerina and Lepiota carry
  amatoxins. A single cap can kill an adult.
- Symptoms are DELAYED, usually 6 to 12 hours after eating, long
  after the poison is absorbed. By the time you feel sick, the toxin
  is already in you.
- There is a FALSE RECOVERY: violent gut symptoms ease for a day or
  two, the person seems better, then liver and kidney failure sets
  in and worsens. Do not trust the calm middle.

== READ THE CLOCK: ONSET TIME IS YOUR BEST CLUE ==
Note the exact time the mushroom was eaten and the exact time
symptoms start. The gap tells you how much danger you are in.
- Symptoms in under 6 hours (often under 2): usually a less-deadly
  gut-irritant type. Miserable vomiting and diarrhea, but most
  people recover with fluids and rest. This is the more common case.
- Symptoms first appearing AFTER 6 hours: treat as a true emergency.
  Delayed onset points to organ-killers (amatoxin = liver,
  orellanine = kidneys days later, gyromitrin, Amanita smithiana).
- Warning: if several kinds were eaten, an early gut upset does NOT
  rule out a delayed killer hiding underneath. When unsure, assume
  the worst and manage aggressively.

== IF SOMEONE ATE A WILD MUSHROOM: FIELD MANAGEMENT ==
Do these yourself, now, whether or not help is reachable. Feeling
fine early means nothing.

FIRST STEPS
- Stop all further eating of the mushroom. Keep the person calm,
  resting, and warm.
- SAVE A SAMPLE: keep any uneaten pieces, dry them in a paper bag
  (not plastic), and photograph the mushroom where it grew including
  the dug-up base. If you ever reach help, this speeds ID.
- Do NOT force vomiting. Once symptoms appear the toxin is already
  absorbed, and forced vomiting risks choking, especially if the
  person is drowsy or confused. If they vomit on their own, let them.

REHYDRATE HARD (this is the main thing you can do)
- The early killer is not the toxin itself but the water and salt
  lost to relentless vomiting and diarrhea. Replacing it is the
  single most useful field action and can keep someone alive for
  days while their body fights.
- Make an oral rehydration drink with disinfected water:
    1 liter clean water
    6 level teaspoons sugar
    half teaspoon salt
  Stir well. No spoons? A rough pinch of salt and a small palm of
  sugar per liter. Any clean sugar-and-salt water beats plain water.
- Give small sips constantly, even between vomiting. Aim to replace
  every watery stool and every vomit with at least an equal volume.
  This may mean several liters a day. Keep going.
- Watch for dangerous dehydration: dizziness when sitting up, dark
  or no urine, fast weak pulse, sunken eyes, confusion. These mean
  push fluids harder and rest the person flat with legs raised.

ACTIVATED CHARCOAL (only if you carry it)
- If you have activated charcoal in your kit and the person is awake
  and can swallow, give it. It may bind toxin still in the gut and
  interrupt the toxin's recycling through the liver.
- Adult dose about 50 grams (or per the package), mixed with water.
  If they tolerate it, repeat a smaller dose every 4 to 6 hours for
  a day or two, as long as they can drink and are alert.
- Never force charcoal into someone who is drowsy, vomiting hard, or
  seizing. Choking on it is a real risk. It is a helper, not a cure.

THROUGH THE FALSE CALM
- If symptoms ease after a day, keep treating anyway: keep fluids up,
  keep resting, keep watching. With amatoxin the quiet phase is when
  the liver is being destroyed silently. Do not resume activity or
  assume victory.

MANAGE WHAT YOU SEE
- Heavy drooling, sweating, tears, tight breathing, tiny pupils:
  points to a muscarine type. Keep the airway clear, sit them up,
  keep fluids going. Usually eases in hours.
- Agitation, hallucination, confusion: protect them from falls, keep
  them calm in a safe spot, do not restrain roughly. Most such cases
  pass on their own.
- Seizures: clear hard objects away, cushion the head, turn them on
  their side after, do not put anything in the mouth.

WARN EVERYONE WHO ATE
- Anyone who shared the meal is at risk and must start the same
  fluids and watching, even with no symptoms yet. Whole families
  have been poisoned from one pot.

EVACUATE IF YOU CAN SAFELY REACH HELP
- If self-rescue or signaling is realistic without endangering the
  group, move toward help while continuing fluids. A hospital can
  give IV fluids, IV silibinin, and N-acetylcysteine, and in the
  worst cases a liver transplant. None of that exists in the field.
- Back in phone coverage, call US Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222.
  It is free, staffed 24/7, and can guide the rest.
- Do not gamble the whole party on a dangerous evacuation. A steady,
  well-hydrated patient in camp can outlast a risky forced march.

== THE HARD TRUTH: NO FIELD CURE FOR THE DEADLY ONES ==
- Be honest with yourself and the patient. For amatoxin (liver) and
  orellanine (kidney) poisoning there is NO antidote you can carry or
  improvise. Fluids and charcoal buy time and fight the dehydration
  that would kill first, but they do not remove the toxin or repair
  a failing organ.
- Do NOT trust folk cures. Milk thistle capsules, herbal teas,
  charcoal alone, "sweating it out," or drinking oil will NOT
  neutralize amatoxin. Believing a false cure wastes the fluid-and-
  rest effort that actually helps and gets people killed.
- A real death-cap dose is often fatal even with the best field
  care and no way out. Say so plainly. Then give the best care
  anyway: aggressive fluids, rest, warmth, charcoal if you have it,
  and constant attention. People have survived. Comfort and dignity
  matter even when odds are bad.

== THE RULE, RESTATED ==
- On the trail, do not eat wild mushrooms. Ever. The surest cure is
  the one bite you never take.
