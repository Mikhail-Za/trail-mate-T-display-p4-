POISON HEMLOCK: DO NOT EAT

Poison hemlock (Conium maculatum) is a deadly member of the carrot
family found across North America. All parts are toxic. It is often
confused with edible wild plants, and eating it can be fatal.

== IDENTIFICATION ==
- Tall, often 4 to 8 feet or more in its second year.
- Stem is smooth, hollow, hairless, and marked with distinct
  purple or reddish-purple blotches and streaks.
- Leaves are fern-like, finely divided, and lacy, resembling
  parsley or carrot tops.
- Flowers are small and white in loose, umbrella-shaped clusters
  (compound umbels).
- Crushed leaves and stems give off an unpleasant musty smell,
  sometimes described as mousy. Do not crush it to test; a whiff
  is not worth handling a deadly plant.
- Grows in disturbed ground, roadsides, ditches, fields, and along
  streams.

== HOW IT KILLS ==
- The toxin coniine paralyzes the nervous system.
- Symptoms include burning mouth, nausea, weakness, trembling,
  slowed heartbeat, then progressive paralysis and death from
  respiratory failure.
- There is no antidote; treatment is emergency hospital support.
  Any suspected ingestion is a life-threatening emergency.

== POISON HEMLOCK vs WATER HEMLOCK ==
- Both are deadly and both have white umbel flowers. Learn to avoid
  both rather than to tell edible from deadly.
- Poison hemlock: purple BLOTCHES on a tall smooth stem, fern-like
  lacy leaves, musty smell, often very tall.
- Water hemlock: purple STREAKING, shorter and stouter, coarser
  toothed leaflets, chambered root. See the water hemlock file.
- Because these and their harmless look-alikes overlap, the safe
  rule is to avoid every wild white-umbel plant entirely.

== SOCRATES NOTE ==
- Poison hemlock is the plant traditionally said to have killed the
  philosopher Socrates in ancient Greece. Remember: hemlock has
  ended lives for thousands of years. Give it the same respect.

== HANDLING WARNING ==
- Do not handle poison hemlock with cuts or broken skin; toxins can
  be absorbed through wounds.
- Wear gloves if you must clear it, wash afterward, and never burn
  it, since smoke can carry irritants. Keep children and pets away.
- Never let the hollow stems be used as toy whistles or straws;
  this has poisoned children.
