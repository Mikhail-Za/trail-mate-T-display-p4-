POISON HEMLOCK: DO NOT EAT

Poison hemlock (Conium maculatum) is a deadly member of the
carrot family found across North America. All parts are toxic.
It is often confused with edible wild plants, and eating even a
small amount can be fatal. There is no antidote. Out here, the
plant kills in one specific way (it stops breathing), and that
single fact is what your whole response is built around.

== IDENTIFICATION (YOUR REAL DEFENSE) ==
Not eating it is the only sure protection. Learn it cold:
- Tall, often 4 to 8 feet or more in its second year. First
  year it is a low rosette of lacy leaves.
- Stem is smooth, hollow, hairless, and marked with distinct
  purple or reddish-purple blotches and streaks.
- Leaves are fern-like, finely divided, and lacy, resembling
  parsley or carrot tops.
- Flowers are small and white in loose, umbrella-shaped
  clusters (compound umbels).
- Crushed leaves and stems give off an unpleasant musty smell,
  sometimes called mousy. Do not crush it to test. A whiff is
  not worth handling a deadly plant.
- Grows in disturbed ground, roadsides, ditches, fields, and
  along streams.
Rule of survival: never eat any wild white-umbel plant. The
deadly ones and the harmless ones overlap too much to sort by
eye, and the price of a mistake is death.

== WHY IT KILLS (AND WHY THAT MATTERS) ==
- The toxins are coniine and gamma-coniceine. They jam the
  signal between nerves and muscle.
- Effects come in two waves. Early: burning mouth, drooling,
  nausea and vomiting, sweating, tremor, twitching muscles,
  restlessness, a FAST heartbeat. Later: weakness spreading
  through the body, drooping eyelids, a SLOW heartbeat, then
  paralysis. Death comes when the breathing muscles stop.
- Onset is usually 30 minutes to 2 hours, sometimes as fast as
  15 minutes, occasionally delayed up to 4 hours.
- The key fact: the poison is temporary. If you can keep the
  person BREATHING through the paralysis, the body clears the
  toxin over hours to a few days and full recovery is possible.
  In a hospital this is done with a ventilator. In the field,
  your lungs and hands are the only ventilator there is.

== FIRST MINUTES AFTER SWALLOWING ==
Do these now, in order:
- Stop all intake. Spit out anything in the mouth. Rinse the
  mouth with water and spit it out. Do not swallow more.
- Do NOT force vomiting. No finger down the throat, no salt
  water, no ipecac. Weakness and seizures come on fast, and a
  person who vomits while weak breathes it into the lungs and
  drowns. This is a top killer, and it is preventable.
- Do NOT pour food or water into them to "dilute" it. That is a
  myth and it raises the choking risk. Small sips only if they
  are fully alert and not nauseated, and stop at once if they
  get drowsy or weak.
- Sit or lie them down and keep them calm and still. Exertion
  and panic speed the poison through the body.
- If you happen to carry activated charcoal and the person is
  wide awake and able to swallow safely, give it early (adult
  dose about 50 grams). Be honest: it only helps in the first
  hour, the evidence is weak, and almost no one carries it. It
  is a bonus, not the plan.
- Note the time they ate it and watch them without a break. The
  whole game is catching the breathing failure the second it
  starts.

== IF BREATHING FAILS: RESCUE BREATHING ==
This is the one action that actually saves a hemlock victim in
the field. It is the field version of the hospital ventilator.
- Watch for: breaths slowing, going shallow, lips or fingertips
  turning gray or blue, or the chest barely moving.
- If breathing stops or nearly stops, start rescue breaths:
  tilt the head back, lift the chin to open the airway, pinch
  the nose, seal your mouth over theirs, give one breath about
  every 5 seconds (roughly 10 to 12 a minute). Watch the chest
  rise with each breath.
- If a barrier mask or a bag-valve-mask is in the kit, use it.
  Same rate.
- If the heart also stops (no pulse, no response), do full CPR:
  30 chest compressions to 2 breaths.
- Do not stop. You may have to keep this up for many hours as
  the toxin wears off. Trade off with others every few minutes
  so no one collapses. This is exhausting, and it is the price
  of keeping them alive.
- People have made full recoveries from severe hemlock
  poisoning purely on supported breathing. It works because the
  poison passes. Your job is to outlast it.

== KEEPING THEM ALIVE WHILE IT PASSES ==
Between breaths and over the long hours, manage the rest:
- Airway first. If they are breathing on their own but drowsy,
  weak, or vomiting, roll them onto their side (recovery
  position) so vomit and drool drain out, not down the windpipe.
  Sweep the mouth clear with a finger.
- Seizures: do not pin them down and do not put anything in the
  mouth. Clear hard or sharp objects away, cushion the head,
  and let it run. When it stops, put them on their side and
  check that they are breathing.
- Keep them warm and flat, protected from cold ground and wind.
  Shock and cold make everything worse.
- Muscle breakdown can injure the kidneys in a survivor. Once
  they are fully alert and keeping fluids down, give steady
  small sips of water over the coming hours to keep them
  hydrated. Never force fluids into someone who is not fully
  awake.
- Expect a long haul. Mild cases ease in 1 to 2 hours. Severe
  ones can take 24 to 72 hours, sometimes days, before strength
  returns. Keep watching for a breathing relapse the whole time.

== EVACUATION IS ONE OPTION, NOT THE PLAN ==
- A ventilator and IV fluids are the definitive treatment, so
  if you can safely reach help, reaching it is worth doing.
  Treat evacuation as one of your options, not the whole answer.
- But breathing support starts NOW, with you, not when you
  arrive somewhere. Do not stand around waiting for a rescue
  that may be hours or days away, or may never come, while the
  person suffocates. Support the breathing first. Move toward
  help only if it does not mean abandoning that support.
- If you are ALONE and realize you ate it: your only window is
  the early phase, before weakness sets in. Move toward people
  or help immediately, while you still can walk and talk. Be
  honest with yourself: once paralysis takes hold and you are
  alone, there is no move left that saves you. No one can
  rescue-breathe themselves. This is exactly why the rule up top
  (never eat a wild white umbel) is the part that keeps you
  alive.

== THE HONEST LIMITS ==
- There is no antidote and no herb, charcoal, food, or trick
  that neutralizes the poison. Anyone who tells you otherwise is
  guessing with your life.
- Supported breathing is real and it works, but doing it by
  hand for hours is brutally hard and may fail, especially with
  one rescuer or none. The outcome is far better with several
  people trading off.
- Prevention beats all of it. This plant has no safe use in the
  field. Do not eat it, do not test it, do not risk it.

== POISON HEMLOCK vs WATER HEMLOCK ==
- Both are deadly and both have white umbel flowers. Learn to
  avoid both rather than to tell edible from deadly.
- Poison hemlock: purple BLOTCHES on a tall smooth stem,
  fern-like lacy leaves, musty smell, often very tall.
- Water hemlock: purple STREAKING or mottling, shorter and
  stouter, coarser toothed leaflets that are less lacy, and a
  chambered root with cross-partitions. Its poison (cicutoxin)
  causes violent SEIZURES fast, which is managed differently.
  See the water hemlock file.
- Because these and their harmless look-alikes overlap, the
  safe rule is to avoid every wild white-umbel plant entirely.

== HANDLING WARNING ==
- Do not handle poison hemlock with cuts or broken skin. The
  toxins can enter through wounds and through the eyes, nose,
  and mouth. Touching intact skin rarely causes a rash, but do
  not gamble.
- Wear gloves if you must clear it, wash hands afterward before
  eating or touching your face, and never burn it, since smoke
  and crushing fumes can carry the toxin. Keep children and
  pets away.
- Never let the hollow stems be used as toy whistles or straws.
  This has poisoned and killed children.

== SOCRATES NOTE ==
- Poison hemlock is the plant traditionally said to have killed
  the philosopher Socrates in ancient Greece: he died of
  creeping paralysis as his breathing failed, the same way it
  kills today. It has ended lives for thousands of years. Give
  it that respect.
