IF SOMEONE IS POISONED BY A PLANT

Stay calm and act. Plant and mushroom poisonings can be serious even
when the person seems fine at first. These steps apply in the field,
away from immediate help. When life-threatening signs appear, treat
it as an emergency and evacuate at once.

== FIRST STEPS ==
- STOP eating. Remove any remaining plant or mushroom from the
  mouth. Do not let the person eat any more.
- Rinse the mouth with clean water and have them spit it out.
- Check breathing and alertness. Seizures, trouble breathing,
  fainting, chest pain, or unresponsiveness are life-threatening.

== SAVE EVIDENCE ==
- Keep a SAMPLE of the plant or mushroom, including leaves, berries,
  and the base or root if you can gather it safely.
- Take clear PHOTOS of the plant where it grew.
- Note WHAT was eaten, HOW MUCH, and WHEN. Identifying the plant
  guides the right treatment.

== WHAT NOT TO DO ==
- Do NOT induce vomiting. It can cause choking and further harm and
  usually does not help.
- Do NOT give salt water, syrup of ipecac, charcoal, or any home
  remedy unless a medical professional directs you.
- Do NOT wait for symptoms with mushrooms or the deadly plants in
  this guide; some toxins are silent for hours.

== KEEP THEM STABLE ==
- If the person is alert and not vomiting, give small sips of plain
  water. Do not force fluids.
- Keep them calm, resting, and warm. Watch them closely and do not
  leave them alone.
- If they vomit, turn them on their side to keep the airway clear.
- If unresponsive but breathing, place on their side (recovery
  position) and monitor.

== TRACK THE TIMELINE ==
- Write down the time of ingestion and the time each symptom starts:
  nausea, vomiting, drooling, sweating, dizziness, confusion,
  vision changes, racing or slow heartbeat, weakness, seizures.
- With some toxins symptoms EASE and then return worse. A false
  recovery is not real recovery; keep heading toward help.
- This timeline is critical information for medics and doctors.

== EVACUATE AND CALL FOR HELP ==
- Move toward help and phone coverage. For serious signs, send for
  rescue if you cannot self-evacuate.
- POISON CONTROL (United States): 1-800-222-1222. Free, staffed
  24/7. It works once you are back in phone coverage; program it
  into your phone before the trip.
- For emergencies with breathing or consciousness problems, call
  your local emergency number (911 in the US) when you have signal.
- Bring the sample, photos, and your timeline notes to the medics.
