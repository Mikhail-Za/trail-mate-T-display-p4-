IF SOMEONE IS POISONED BY A PLANT

You may be hours or days from help, or on your own for good.
Most plant and mushroom poisonings are survived by good field
care: protect the airway, replace lost fluids, and match your
actions to the signs. Start now. Do not wait for symptoms;
some deadly toxins are silent for many hours.

== ACT NOW (FIRST MINUTES) ==
- Stop the exposure. Get any plant or mushroom out of the
  mouth. No one eats any more, not even a "test" bite.
- Rinse the mouth with clean water and spit it out.
- Check the person: Are they awake? Breathing normally? Can
  they swallow and talk?
- Danger signs: seizures, trouble breathing, blue lips,
  fainting, chest pain, or no response. Treat as dire and
  do everything below at once.

== KNOW THE CLOCK (THIS GUIDES EVERYTHING) ==
- Fast onset, under 6 hours: usually GI upset, sweating, or
  a wild mental state. Serious but most survive with care.
- Slow onset, 6 hours or more before the first symptom: this
  is the danger pattern. Amatoxin mushrooms (destroying
  angel, death cap) and some plants hide, then hit the liver
  or kidneys. Assume the worst and manage hard.
- FALSE RECOVERY is real. Symptoms can ease for a day, then
  return far worse as organs fail. Feeling better is NOT
  proof of safety. Keep treating and keep watching.

== KEEP THEM ALIVE ON YOUR OWN ==
This is the core of field survival. Do all that apply.
- Airway first. If they vomit or pass out, roll them onto
  their side (recovery position) so they cannot choke.
  Sweep the mouth clear. Never leave them alone.
- FIGHT DEHYDRATION. Vomiting and diarrhea kill by draining
  fluid and salts, especially with mushrooms. Replacing that
  loss is the single most useful thing you can do without a
  hospital.
  Field rehydration drink (per 1 liter clean water):
    - 6 level teaspoons sugar
    - one half teaspoon salt
    - stir until dissolved
  Give small sips often, every few minutes. Small and
  frequent stays down better than big gulps. Match what
  goes out: the more they vomit or purge, the more they
  must sip. If you have no salt, still give the sugar
  water and plain clean water; it helps less but helps.
  NEVER use wood ash or campfire ash: wet ash is caustic
  lye and will burn the mouth and gut. Salty food you
  carry (bouillon, jerky, salted crackers) safely adds
  the salt if you have it.
- Rest and warmth. Lay them down, shade or shelter, keep
  them from chilling. Minimize exertion; do not make a sick
  heart or liver work harder than it must.
- Reassure them. Fear and panic worsen fast heartbeat and
  hallucinations. A calm voice and a calm camp help.

== ACTIVATED CHARCOAL (ONLY IF YOU CARRY IT) ==
Charcoal can bind many toxins and is a real field tool, but
only under strict limits. It is not a cure and does little
once hours have passed.
- Give ONLY if the person is fully awake, alert, and can
  swallow and protect their own airway.
- Best within about 1 hour of eating the plant. Later gives
  little benefit.
- Adult dose: about 25 to 50 grams stirred into water.
- Do NOT give if: drowsy, confused, seizing, vomiting
  uncontrollably, or if they swallowed a caustic (burning)
  or fuel/solvent-type substance. In those cases it can
  choke them or make things worse.
- This is pharmacy or medical activated charcoal. Campfire
  charcoal or briquettes are NOT a substitute.

== MATCH YOUR CARE TO THE SIGNS ==
You may not know the plant. Treat the pattern you see.

WET AND DROOLING (sweating, drooling, tearing, watery gut,
slow pulse; certain small mushrooms, the Inocybe and
Clitocybe types):
- Keep the airway clear; expect heavy secretions and vomit.
- Side position, sweep the mouth often, rehydrate hard.
- Most survive with airway care and fluids. The true fix
  (atropine) is hospital-only, but death is uncommon.

HOT, DRY, AND WILD (dry mouth, big pupils, flushed dry skin,
racing heart, fever, agitation, seeing things; jimsonweed,
angel's trumpet, nightshade, henbane):
- Cool them down: shade, remove extra layers, wet the skin
  and fan it. Overheating is a top killer here.
- Keep them safe from themselves: calm, dim, quiet spot;
  clear away hazards; hold or pad them gently if thrashing.
- Sip fluids when they can swallow safely.
- Symptoms can last 1 to 2 days. Many pull through on
  supportive care alone. Ride it out and protect them.

SLOW OR ERRATIC HEARTBEAT, FAINTING (nausea then a slow,
skipping, or pounding pulse; weakness; foxglove, oleander,
lily of the valley):
- Absolute rest, flat, no walking or exertion. Exertion can
  trigger a fatal rhythm.
- Sip fluids only if fully alert. Watch breathing and pulse.
- Be honest: the antidote is hospital-only. Rest and calm
  buy time; evacuate by carry, not by making them walk.

DELAYED GUT STORM AFTER 6+ HOURS (cramps, vomiting, watery
"cholera-like" diarrhea starting many hours after eating a
mushroom): treat as possible amatoxin, the deadliest group.
- Rehydrate aggressively and continuously (see the drink
  above). Dehydration and shock are what kill first; fluids
  are your one real weapon in the field.
- Expect false recovery, then possible liver failure.
- Straight truth: there is no field cure. The definitive
  drugs (silibinin, NAC) and liver support exist only in
  hospitals. Fluids maximize the odds of lasting long
  enough to reach one. If none exists, aggressive fluids
  and comfort remain the best and most humane care you can
  give.

RAPID COLLAPSE, GASPING, ALMOND-LIKE SMELL (very fast onset;
some seeds, pits, and cassava-type roots hold cyanide):
- There is no field antidote. Fresh air, keep the airway
  open, rescue breathing if trained and they stop.
- This is a true emergency with no self-reliant fix. Do the
  basics and get help if it exists.

== WHAT NOT TO DO ==
- Do NOT force vomiting. It rarely helps and can cause
  choking or burns on the way back up. Ipecac is obsolete.
- Do NOT give salt water, milk, or campfire charcoal as a
  "cure." These do not help and can harm.
- Do NOT chug large amounts of water to "dilute" the poison.
  That is different from sipping the rehydration drink to
  replace fluids they are losing. Sip to replace, do not
  flush to dilute.
- Do NOT trust a false recovery and stop caring for them.
- Do NOT eat an unknown plant again to "confirm" it.

== SAVE EVIDENCE AND TRACK THE TIMELINE ==
Useful for your own decisions and vital if you do reach help.
- Keep a SAMPLE: leaves, berries, cap, and the base or root
  if you can gather it safely without more exposure.
- Take clear PHOTOS of the plant where it grew.
- Write the time it was eaten, how much, and the time each
  symptom starts: nausea, vomiting, drooling, sweating,
  dizziness, confusion, vision changes, fast or slow pulse,
  weakness, seizures.
- Note whether symptoms started before or after 6 hours.
  That single fact tells medics how worried to be.

== IF YOU CAN REACH HELP ==
Evacuation is one option among the actions above, not the
whole plan. Keep doing the field care while you try.
- Evacuate only if you can do it safely. For a weak, faint,
  or heart-affected person, carry or shelter in place and
  send others for rescue rather than making them walk.
- POISON CONTROL (United States): 1-800-222-1222. Free,
  staffed 24/7, works the moment you regain phone signal.
  Program it before the trip.
- For breathing or consciousness emergencies, call your
  local emergency number (911 in the US) when you have
  signal.
- Bring the sample, photos, and timeline to the medics.

== THE HARD TRUTH ==
For the deadliest exposures (amatoxin liver failure, severe
foxglove or oleander, cyanide), no field remedy replaces a
hospital. Anyone who promises a wilderness "cure" for these
is wrong, and following them can kill. What you CAN do is
real and it matters: protect the airway, replace lost fluids,
prevent overheating, enforce rest, and stay with them. That
care carries many people through, and for the rest it gives
them the best and most dignified chance you have to offer.
