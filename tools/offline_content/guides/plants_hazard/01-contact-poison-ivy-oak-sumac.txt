POISON IVY, OAK, AND SUMAC

These three plants carry urushiol, an oil that causes an itchy,
blistering rash in most people. You do not need to eat them to be
harmed; contact is enough. Learn them, then keep your distance.

== LEAVES OF THREE ==
- "Leaves of three, let it be" is a useful start but NOT complete.
- Poison ivy and poison oak usually show three leaflets per stem.
- Poison sumac does NOT follow this rule (see below).
- Many harmless plants also have three leaflets, so treat three-
  leaflet plants as suspect, not as proof.

== TELLING THEM APART ==
- Poison ivy: three pointed leaflets, middle leaflet on a longer
  stalk; edges smooth, toothed, or lobed; grows as a vine or low
  shrub; often a hairy "furry rope" vine on trees; found across
  most of North America.
- Poison oak: three leaflets with rounded, lobed edges resembling
  oak leaves; shrub or vine; more common in the West and Southeast.
- Poison sumac: 7 to 13 leaflets in pairs along a reddish stem;
  a shrub or small tree of wet swamps and bogs in the East.
- All three can show small white or cream berries in clusters.
  White berries on a woody plant are a strong warning sign.

== HOW URUSHIOL SPREADS ==
- The oil clings to skin, clothing, tools, packs, and dog fur.
- Petting a dog that walked through it can give you the rash.
- The oil stays active on gear for months if not washed off.
- NEVER burn these plants. Smoke carries urushiol into the eyes,
  throat, and lungs and can cause a dangerous internal reaction.

== WASHING TIMELINE ==
- Wash exposed skin as soon as possible, ideally within 30 minutes.
- Use soap and cool water, or an alcohol wipe, then rinse well.
- Wash tools, boots, and clothing separately; wear gloves.
- Rinse the dog before it spreads oil to everyone.

== RASH AND TREATMENT ==
- Rash appears in 12 to 72 hours: redness, swelling, blisters,
  intense itch, often in streaks where the plant brushed skin.
- The rash fluid does NOT spread the rash; new patches come from
  more oil, not from scratching open blisters.
- Cool compresses, calamine, oatmeal baths, and antihistamines
  ease symptoms. Do not scratch; broken skin can get infected.
- Seek care for face or genital involvement, widespread rash,
  breathing trouble, or signs of infection.

== BLACK-DOT SIGN ==
- Urushiol turns black on exposure to air.
- Small black dots or streaks on a leaf can mark these plants.
- Never handle a suspect plant to look for this; observe only.

== SEASON ==
- The urushiol oil is active YEAR-ROUND: bare winter stems and
  leafless vines still cause the rash, so do not grab an unknown
  hairy vine for a handhold in any season.
- Spring and summer bring the most exposure from green leaves.
- In fall the leaflets often turn bright red or orange; do not
  reach for the colorful foliage.
