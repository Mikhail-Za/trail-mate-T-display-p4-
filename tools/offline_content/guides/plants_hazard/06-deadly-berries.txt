DEADLY AND TOXIC BERRIES

Wild berries are a common cause of poisoning, especially in
children. Color is not a safe guide. Do not eat any wild berry
unless you are certain of its ID. When in doubt, do not eat it.

== POKEWEED ==
- Tall plant, up to 10 feet, with smooth reddish-purple stems.
- Dark purple berries hang in long drooping clusters (racemes).
- Roots, stems, leaves, and raw berries are toxic; causes severe
  vomiting and cramps. Children are drawn to the shiny berries.
- Do not eat. The young cooked greens have a food tradition but
  require repeated careful boiling; not a field food.

== NIGHTSHADES ==
- Bittersweet nightshade: vine with purple star-shaped flowers with
  yellow centers; berries turn from green to bright red, in
  clusters. Toxic.
- Black nightshade: small white star flowers; small round black
  berries. Some forms are eaten cooked when fully ripe, but green
  berries and leaves are toxic and look-alikes are risky. Treat all
  wild nightshade berries as unsafe.

== BANEBERRY ==
- White baneberry ("doll's-eyes"): shiny white berries, each with a
  dark spot, on thick red stalks. Red baneberry has red berries.
- The whole plant, especially berries, is highly toxic and can
  affect the heart. A few berries can seriously harm a child.

== MOONSEED vs WILD GRAPE ==
- Moonseed is a toxic vine that mimics wild grape; it can kill.
- The reliable difference is the SEED: moonseed has a single flat,
  crescent or moon-shaped seed; true grapes have one to a few
  round, pear-shaped seeds.
- Moonseed leaves lack the grape's tendrils and have smooth (not
  toothed) edges. Do not rely on leaf shape alone.
- Never taste to decide. Split a berry and check the seed shape; if
  crescent, or if you are unsure, do not eat it.

== VIRGINIA CREEPER ==
- A common climbing vine with FIVE leaflets per stem (unlike poison
  ivy's three) and dark blue-black berries on red stalks.
- The berries are toxic to people and cause mouth and stomach
  irritation. Do not eat them.

== HOLLY AND YEW ==
- Holly: spiny evergreen leaves and bright red berries; berries
  cause vomiting and are toxic if several are eaten.
- Yew: evergreen with flat needles and a red berry-like aril. The
  soft red flesh is not the main danger; the hard SEED inside, plus
  the needles and bark, are highly poisonous and can stop the
  heart. Never eat any part of a yew, and keep children away.

== RULE ==
- No wild berry goes in the mouth without certain ID. Teach
  children never to eat wild berries.
