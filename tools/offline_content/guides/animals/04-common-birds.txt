Common Birds and What They Tell You

== WHY BIRDS MATTER ==
- Birds are easy to see and they read the landscape for you: they mark water, weather, and sometimes danger.
- A few ground birds are realistic survival food. Most are legally protected and are watched, not caught, except in a true emergency.

== WATERFOWL AND WATERBIRDS ==
- Ducks, geese, and herons mean open water nearby. Geese fly in long V lines; herons stand motionless in the shallows.
- Grain and seed eaters (doves, finches) fly low and straight toward water at dawn and dusk, then wander erratically once they have drunk. Follow the straight, purposeful dawn flights to find water.
- Kingfishers and swallows hunt over water and rarely stray far from it.

== RAPTORS ==
- Hawks and eagles soar on broad wings; falcons have pointed wings and fast, snapping flapping. Vultures rock side to side on raised wings and circle over carrion.
- A tight circle of vultures dropping down marks a dead animal, which can also mean a predator is feeding there. Note it and avoid it.
- Owls hunt at night. A daytime mob of small birds screaming at one spot often means a roosting owl or hawk.

== GROUND BIRDS ==
- Grouse: chicken-sized, brown, explode into flight from underfoot with a startling roar. Often tame enough to approach closely.
- Wild turkey: large, dark, long-legged, travels in flocks and roosts in trees at night.
- Quail: small, round, run in coveys; ptarmigan turn white in winter in the far north and high mountains.
- These are the classic survival birds. Taking them is emergency-only and regulated; see the trapping and preparing articles.

== CROWS, JAYS, AND SONGBIRDS ==
- Crows and ravens are smart, loud, and notice everything. A sudden uproar can mean a predator, a carcass, or that you have been spotted.
- Jays scold intruders and will announce your movement through the woods.
- Songbirds falling silent all at once can mean a hawk just passed over.

== BIRDS AS CLUES ==
- Water: converging low flights of seed eaters at dawn and dusk point toward it.
- Direction and time: many birds sing hardest at first light, giving you a rough east and a rough clock.
- Weather: birds feeding heavily and flying low often come before a storm; high soaring on calm thermals suggests fair weather holding. Treat these as hints, not forecasts.

== SEASON ==
- Spring and fall migration fill wetlands with waterfowl and are the best times to find birds concentrated at water.
- In spring, birds are nesting. A bird faking a broken wing is luring you away from its nest; leave the area and neither of you wastes the effort.

== CAUTION ==
- Most birds, their nests, and their eggs are legally protected. Watching them and using them as clues is always fine. Taking them is a true-emergency exception covered in the food articles.
