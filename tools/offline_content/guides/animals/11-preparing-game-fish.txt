Preparing Wild Game and Fish Safely

== THE CORE RULE ==
- Wild meat and fish can carry parasites and bacteria. Cook everything thoroughly: game until the juices run clear and there is no pink at the bone, fish until it is opaque and flakes apart. Heat is your safety.
- Never eat wild game or fish raw or rare in a survival setting. The parasite risk is real, and thorough cooking removes most of it.

== WHAT NOT TO EAT ==
- Any animal found already dead. You do not know what killed it or how long ago, and it may be diseased or already rotten.
- Roadkill is a gamble: unknown time dead, internal injury spoiling the meat, and warmth all work against you. Only fresh and still-cold is worth considering, and even then cook it hard.
- Any animal that behaved strangely, looked sick, was staggering, or shows sores, tumors, swollen or spotted organs, foul smell, or a bad-looking liver. When the liver looks wrong, discard the whole animal.
- Predators and scavengers concentrate parasites and disease. Plant eaters such as rabbit, squirrel, and deer are the safer survival choice.

== FIELD DRESSING ==
- Gut the animal as soon as possible: open the belly carefully without cutting the intestines, remove the innards, and keep the meat clean and cool.
- Cool the meat fast in shade, moving air, or cold water; warmth spoils it within hours, while cold and dry preserves it.
- Keep hair, dirt, and gut contents off the meat. Rinse with clean water if you have it.
- Wash your hands and knife after handling the raw carcass; do not then touch food you plan to eat cold.

== SPECIFIC HAZARDS ==
- Rabbits can carry tularemia, a bacterial disease. Be extra careful in warm months, cover any cuts on your hands while skinning, and cook rabbit especially well. Avoid any rabbit that was slow, tame, or spotted with sores.
- Bear and wild pig meat can carry trichinella worms; cook it well done throughout, never pink.
- Freshwater fish carry flukes and parasites; cook until fully opaque and flaking.
- Waterfowl and scavenging birds should be cooked thoroughly like any other wild meat.

== COOKING ==
- Boiling is the most efficient survival method: it cooks meat fully, saves the broth and its calories, and does not need constant tending.
- If roasting over a fire, keep the pieces small so the center actually cooks. A charred outside over raw meat is how people get sick.
- When in doubt, cook it longer. There is no penalty for overcooking survival meat and a serious one for undercooking it.

== SEASON ==
- Warm weather spoils meat within hours and raises disease risk, tularemia in rabbits especially. Eat it fresh, cook it hard, and do not keep leftovers you cannot hold cold.
- Cold weather is your refrigerator: a clean, gutted carcass keeps for days hung in freezing air, which is when preserving a larger catch becomes realistic.

== CAUTION ==
- This is emergency food handling, not a butchery course. When any sign says the animal was sick, the safe choice is to go hungry and move on. Starvation takes weeks; a bad meal can drop you in a day.
