Common Mammals of North America

== HOW TO USE THIS ==
- These are the mammals you are most likely to see. Most are harmless at a distance and want nothing to do with you.
- The genuinely dangerous large mammals (bears, moose, cougars, wolves) have their own articles in the Nature section. This article covers the common, mostly harmless ones.

== DEER AND ELK ==
- Deer: tan to gray-brown, white belly, about 3 feet at the shoulder. White-tailed deer flag a white tail up when fleeing. Mule deer have very large ears and bound stiff-legged on all fours.
- Elk: much larger, up to 5 feet at the shoulder, dark neck, pale rump; bulls bugle in fall.
- Behavior: alert and skittish. They freeze, then bolt. Harmless in general, but a rutting bull or a cornered deer can injure. Keep your distance in fall and around young in spring.

== COYOTE AND FOX ==
- Coyote: like a lean gray-brown medium dog, pointed nose, bushy black-tipped tail carried low when running. Yips and howls at night.
- Red fox: smaller, rusty red, black legs, white tail tip. Gray fox is grizzled gray and can climb trees.
- Behavior: shy and curious. Attacks on people are very rare, and a bold one has usually been fed. Do not run; yell, wave your arms, and look big.

== RABBITS AND HARES ==
- Cottontail rabbit: small, gray-brown, white cotton-ball tail; freezes, then zigzags away.
- Jackrabbit, which is a hare: bigger, huge ears, long legs, favors open country.
- Behavior: harmless. A common survival small game; see the trapping article.

== SQUIRRELS AND CHIPMUNKS ==
- Tree squirrels (gray, fox, red) and ground squirrels are everywhere. Chipmunks are smaller with striped faces and backs.
- Behavior: harmless but bold around camps and will raid food. Do not hand-feed them; they bite and carry fleas.

== RACCOON, SKUNK, AND OPOSSUM ==
- Raccoon: black mask, ringed tail, hand-like front paws. Skunk: black with white stripes, warns by raising its tail and stamping before it sprays. Opossum: pale gray, naked rat-like tail, plays dead when cornered.
- Behavior: mostly nighttime camp raiders. Skunks warn first, so back away and give one room. These species, along with foxes and bats, are the main rabies carriers; see the warning below.

== PORCUPINE, BEAVER, AND MUSKRAT ==
- Porcupine: slow, rounded, covered in quills. It cannot throw them but slaps with a quilled tail. Keep dogs well away.
- Beaver: large, dark, flat scaly tail, slaps the water and dives; look for dammed ponds and gnawed stumps. Muskrat is smaller with a thin tail.
- Behavior: harmless if left alone. Beaver ponds are reliable water and a magnet for other wildlife.

== RABIES WARNING ==
- Raccoons, skunks, foxes, and bats are the main rabies carriers in North America.
- Suspect rabies in any of these active in daytime that is staggering, unafraid, or aggressive for no reason. Never handle, corner, or feed one, including babies. Never touch a bat with bare hands.
- Any bite or scratch from a wild mammal, or waking with a bat in your shelter, needs the wound washed hard and prompt medical care. See the first aid bites and stings article.

== SEASON ==
- Fall rut makes bucks and bull elk unpredictable. Spring means mothers with young; never step between them.
- Many mammals move most at dawn and dusk, which is also when you are most likely to meet one on the trail.
