Harmless and Beneficial Snakes

== WHY LEAVE THEM ==
- The great majority of North American snakes are non-venomous and harmless to you if you do not handle them.
- Snakes eat rodents that raid food and spread disease, plus insects and slugs. A snake near camp is thinning out the mice near camp.
- Killing a snake is the single most common way people get bitten. Leaving it alone is both safer for you and better for the country you are in.

== THE COMMON HARMLESS SNAKES ==
- Garter snakes: small, striped lengthwise down the body, in almost every field, yard, and streamside across the continent. Harmless.
- Rat snakes: long climbers, often black, gray, or yellowish; excellent rodent hunters around barns and cabins.
- Kingsnakes and milk snakes: smooth and glossy, often banded or blotched; they even eat other snakes, including venomous ones.
- Gopher, bull, and pine snakes: large, blotched, living in open and sandy country, and bluffing loudly by hissing and buzzing the tail.
- Water snakes: common in and around every pond and slow river, basking on banks and branches and diving when approached.

== TELLING THEM FROM THE DANGEROUS FEW ==
- Most harmless snakes have a slender head about as wide as the neck, round pupils, and a thin tail with no rattle. The venomous natives are covered in the venomous snakes article; read the two together.
- Do not rely on any one feature. The safe default still applies: if you are not sure, do not handle it, and simply give it room.

== IF ONE IS IN CAMP ==
- Give it space and time; it will almost always leave on its own once no one is crowding it.
- To move one along, tap the ground nearby or gently sweep behind it with a long stick or trekking pole from a safe distance. Never pick it up.
- Store food sealed so you are not feeding the mice that draw the snakes in the first place.

== SEASON ==
- Even harmless snakes are most visible spring through fall, basking on cool mornings and moving at dusk in the heat.
- A snake found coiled and still on a cold morning is only cold, not tame. It will warm and move as the sun reaches it.
