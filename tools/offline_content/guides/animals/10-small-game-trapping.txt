Small Game and Trapping for Survival

== LEGAL AND ETHICAL FIRST ==
- Snares and most traps are illegal for casual use nearly everywhere and are tightly regulated even for licensed trappers. They are a true survival emergency tool only. Know your local law.
- Trapping is for staying alive, not for sport or for practicing on live animals. Learn the knots and trigger mechanisms on a stick at home, never on wildlife.
- Take only what you need, and check your sets often so nothing suffers in a trap longer than it must.

== WHY TRAPS OVER HUNTING ==
- Traps work while you handle other survival tasks: they hunt for you around the clock, which one person actively hunting cannot.
- Set many simple traps rather than one clever one. A line of ten snares beats a day spent chasing a single rabbit.

== WHERE TO SET ==
- Set on active sign, not at random: fresh tracks, droppings, worn runs through grass, gnawed vegetation, and burrow entrances.
- Rabbit and rodent runs, the small tunnels worn through grass and brush, and the pinch points where a trail narrows between obstacles are the best spots.
- Funnel the animal: use sticks to gently narrow a run so it must pass through your set.

== SIMPLE METHODS ==
- Simple snare: a small loop of wire or strong cord set in a run at the head height of the target animal, anchored firmly, so the animal walks in and tightens it. Wire holds an open loop best.
- Baiting: seeds, nuts, greens, or a bright scrap of food at the set can draw in squirrels and rodents.
- Fixed traps such as a weighted deadfall are classic but hard to build well and dangerous to your own fingers. A snare on good sign is usually the better bet.
- Birds: ground birds such as grouse and quail can sometimes be taken with a snare on a run or a baited set. See the birds article for where they travel.

== HUMANE DISPATCH ==
- If you catch an animal alive, kill it quickly and without hesitation. A sharp, firm blow to the back of the head, or breaking the neck on small game, is fastest.
- Hesitation is both cruel and dangerous; a struggling animal bites and scratches. Be committed and be quick.

== SEASON ==
- Animals are in best condition in fall, fattened for winter. Winter snow makes runs and tracks obvious and helps preserve your catch, but animals are leaner and life is harder.
- Spring means young and pregnant animals; avoid taking obvious mothers whenever there is a choice.

== CAUTION: BEFORE YOU EAT IT ==
- Handle carcasses as little as possible with bare hands; wild animals carry fleas, ticks, and disease.
- Do not eat an animal that was found dead, acted sick, or looks diseased. See the preparing article.
- Cook all wild game thoroughly. Full field-dressing and cooking guidance is in the preparing wild game and fish article.
