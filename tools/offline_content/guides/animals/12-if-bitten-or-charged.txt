If Bitten, Stung, or Charged: Quick Actions

== THIS IS THE QUICK CARD ==
- Fast reminders only. The full treatment for every item here is in the Water and First Aid section, bites and stings article. Read that one at home so these prompts are enough in the moment.

== SNAKEBITE ==
- Back everyone away from the snake. Do not chase it; the treatment is the same without an identification.
- Keep the victim calm and still. Remove rings, watches, and tight clothing before swelling starts.
- Keep the bitten limb near heart level and loosely splinted. Mark the swelling edge and write down the time.
- Evacuate to a hospital now. No cutting, no suction, no tourniquet, no ice, no alcohol.

== SPIDER BITE ==
- Wash it, apply a cold compress, note the time.
- Black widow (cramping belly and muscle pain with sweating) or brown recluse (a spreading dark sore over days): evacuate for care. Most other bites just need cleaning and watching for infection.

== BEE, WASP, AND ANAPHYLAXIS ==
- Scrape a bee stinger out sideways at once; do not pinch it. Cold compress for normal local swelling.
- Watch for anaphylaxis: spreading hives, swelling lips or throat, wheezing, dizziness, vomiting. This kills in minutes.
- If it appears, give epinephrine into the outer thigh immediately, repeat in 5 to 15 minutes if no better, and evacuate even if they recover.

== TICK ==
- Remove it with fine tweezers gripped at the skin, pulling straight out, with no twisting or burning. Wash the site and note the date.
- Watch 30 days for an expanding rash or flu-like illness, and seek care if either appears.

== ANIMAL BITE OR SCRATCH ==
- Wash hard with soap and water for many minutes. Do not close the wound. Dress it and get medical care.
- Rabies: any bite or scratch from a bat, raccoon, skunk, fox, coyote, or unknown dog or cat, or waking with a bat in your shelter, needs prompt medical attention. Rabies is fatal once symptoms start, and the shots work only if started early.

== LARGE ANIMAL CHARGE ==
- Bear: stand tall and fight back against a black bear; play dead for a surprised or defensive grizzly, and fight only a predatory one. See the Nature bear article.
- Moose: run and put a tree or rock between you; if knocked down, curl up and protect your head until it leaves.
- Cougar or wolf: do not run, look big, back away, fight back if attacked. Pick up small children.
- The common thread: a predator you face and fight; a moose you flee. Never run from a bear or a cougar.

== THE FIRST STEP IS ALWAYS THE SAME ==
- Stop, get everyone to a safe distance, then treat. Panic and crowding create second victims. Then work through the first aid article.
