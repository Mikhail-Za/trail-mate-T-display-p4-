Bears and Large Animals (See Nature Section)

== THIS IS A POINTER ==
- The large animals that can seriously hurt you have full articles in the Nature section. This is a short reminder so the Animals section is complete. For the detail, read those articles before you travel.

== BEARS ==
- Black bears live across most of the continent; grizzly, or brown, bears live mainly in Alaska, western Canada, and the northern Rockies. Tell them apart by the grizzly's shoulder hump, dished face, and long claws, never by color.
- Prevention is food storage and noise. Response differs by species and situation: stand tall and fight a black bear that makes contact; play dead for a surprised or defensive grizzly, and fight back only if the attack is predatory.
- The full method, including bear spray and camp setup, is in the Nature section bear article.

== MOOSE ==
- Moose injure more people than bears do. They look calm and are not. Give them 50 yards or more.
- Warning signs: ears back, hackles up, head low, lip licking. If a moose charges, run and get a tree or boulder between you. This is the one big animal you should run from.
- Full detail is in the Nature section other wildlife article.

== COUGAR (MOUNTAIN LION) ==
- Rarely seen, rarely attacks. If you meet one: do not run, do not crouch, look big, hold eye contact, back away slowly, and fight back hard if attacked.
- Pick up small children at once, without bending low. Detail is in the Nature section other wildlife article.

== WOLVES AND COYOTES ==
- Attacks on adults are very rare and usually involve animals fed by people. Stand tall, make noise, back away, never run, and fight back if one makes contact.

== THE ONE RULE FOR ALL OF THEM ==
- Distance, food storage, and never running from a predator cover almost every situation. Do not approach or feed any large animal, and never get between one and its young.
