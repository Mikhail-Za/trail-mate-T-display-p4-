Animals: How to Use This Section

== WHAT THIS SECTION IS ==
- A field companion for the animals you may see, avoid, or in a real emergency use for food.
- Text only, no photos on this screen. It is a memory aid for animals you already know, not a way to identify a dangerous or edible animal for the first time.
- Learn the local dangerous species (venomous snakes, spiders, big animals) at home, with photos and ideally a local expert, before you ever need this.

== THE GOLDEN RULES ==
- Observe, do not approach. Distance is your best safety tool and your best chance at a clean identification.
- If you are not certain an animal is harmless, treat it as dangerous. Assume any snake is venomous and any wild mammal can bite until you are sure otherwise.
- Never corner, trap, or surprise an animal. Almost every bite and charge comes from an animal that felt cut off from escape. Always leave it a way out.
- Never get between an animal and its young, its food, or its escape route.
- Do not feed wildlife, ever. A fed animal loses its fear of people and becomes the one that has to be killed later.
- Do not handle dead or sick-acting animals. Reflex bites, rabies, and disease all live here.

== FOOD STORAGE ==
- Your food and scented items (toothpaste, sunscreen, trash) draw everything from mice to bears. Store them sealed and away from where you sleep.
- In bear country use a canister, an approved hang, or a provided locker. See the Nature section bear article for the full method.
- A clean camp is a safe camp. Almost every wildlife problem in camp is really a food problem.

== IF THINGS GO WRONG ==
- For any bite, sting, or animal attack, the treatment lives in the Water and First Aid section, bites and stings article. This section tells you how to avoid the encounter; that one tells you what to do after it.

== HOW THE REST OF THIS SECTION IS ORGANIZED ==
- Reading tracks and sign; common mammals; common birds; venomous snakes and their safe lookalikes; harmless snakes; spiders; bears and large animals; fishing; small game and trapping; preparing wild game and fish; and quick actions if bitten or charged.
- The food articles (fishing, trapping, preparing game) are survival-only and carry legal and safety cautions. Read them before you would ever need them, not during the emergency.
