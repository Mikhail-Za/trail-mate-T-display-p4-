Spiders: Real Risk and Common Harmless Kinds

== KEEP THIS IN PROPORTION ==
- Spider bites are rare, and the great majority of spiders in North America cannot meaningfully hurt you. Two kinds are worth knowing: the black widow and the brown recluse.
- Most sores blamed on spider bites are actually skin infections. Real bites are uncommon and usually happen when a spider is trapped against skin, in clothing, a boot, a glove, or bedding.
- Prevention is simple: shake out boots, clothing, and sleeping gear, and take care reaching into woodpiles, rock walls, and dark corners.

== BLACK WIDOW ==
- Glossy jet-black round body about the size of a large pea, with a red hourglass mark on the underside of the belly. Males and young are smaller and drab and rarely bite.
- Lives across most of the continental US in dark, sheltered spots: woodpiles, rock crevices, sheds, under seats and steps, and in old outhouses.
- Bite: a pinprick, then within an hour cramping muscle pain in the belly, back, or limbs, with sweating. Rarely fatal to a healthy adult but genuinely severe. Evacuate for care.

== BROWN RECLUSE ==
- Sandy brown, long-legged, body under half an inch, with a darker violin shape on the back pointing toward the rear.
- Range is limited, mainly the south-central and lower midwestern states. It is heavily over-reported elsewhere; outside that range a suspected recluse bite is almost always something else.
- Bite: often painless at first, then over days a blister and a dark, sunken sore. Clean it, cover it, and seek care.

== THE HARMLESS MANY ==
- Orb weavers spin the big round webs across trails at face height. Large and startling, but harmless; wave a stick ahead of you on overgrown trails.
- Wolf spiders are big, fast, hairy ground hunters that carry their young on their backs. They look alarming and are essentially harmless.
- Jumping spiders are small, fuzzy, and curious; grass spiders build funnel sheets down in the grass. Neither is a danger to you.
- Daddy longlegs, the harvestmen, are not spiders at all and are not venomous.

== SEASON ==
- Spiders are most active and most often seen from late summer into fall, when they are largest and webs are everywhere.
- Cool weather drives them into sheltered spots, which is exactly when they end up in stored boots, gloves, and bedding. Shake gear out year round, especially after cold nights.

== IF BITTEN ==
- Wash the bite, apply a cold compress, and note the time. Watch for spreading redness, severe cramping, or a worsening sore, and evacuate for the widow and recluse patterns above. See the first aid bites and stings article.
